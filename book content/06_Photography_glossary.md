# Photography Glossary

Photography has a vocabulary for describing light, operating equipment, interpreting pictures and bringing work to an audience. This chapter is a reference to return to as those words become useful. Definitions begin with the everyday meaning, then add technical distinctions where these help explain the photograph.

## Using this glossary

Entries are alphabetical. Each includes a practical example and links to related entries. Alternative names and common abbreviations appear in the entry headings or the [abbreviation index](#abbreviation-index). Examples illustrate a principle; their settings are starting points rather than prescriptions.

Links marked **In the book** lead to fuller discussions in [Chapter 3 — Equipment & Settings](03_equipment_%26_settings.md) or [Chapter 5 — Photography Genres & Careers](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md). Technical terms describe mechanisms, while terms such as documentary, fine art and street photography describe overlapping approaches rather than rigid categories. The [references](#references) identify the educational, manufacturer and standards sources used for terminology and further checking. Definitions and examples are written for this book.

## A

<a id="g-aberration"></a>
### Aberration

An optical imperfection that prevents a lens from forming an ideal image. Different aberrations can soften detail, distort shapes or produce coloured fringes. **Example:** stars near a frame's corners may look like tiny wings because of coma. **See also:** [chromatic aberration](#g-chromatic-aberration), [distortion](#g-distortion), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-aerial-photography"></a>
### Aerial photography

Photography from an elevated airborne position, such as an aircraft or drone. Height, direction and camera angle change how a landscape's patterns become visible. **Example:** a downward view turns fields into a pattern of rectangles. **See also:** [landscape photography](#g-landscape-photography), [viewpoint](#g-viewpoint).

<a id="g-af-area"></a>
### AF area / focus area

The part of the frame in which the camera looks for focus. A small point gives precise placement; wider areas and tracking modes give the camera more choice. **Example:** select a small area to focus through branches onto a bird. **See also:** [autofocus](#g-autofocus), [subject detection](#g-subject-detection). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Focus](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#focus-choosing-what-matters).

<a id="g-albumen-print"></a>
### Albumen print

A photographic print with a silver image held in an egg-white binder on paper. It was a major nineteenth-century printing method. **Example:** a museum label identifies a thin historical photograph mounted on card as an albumen silver print. **See also:** [gelatin-silver print](#g-gelatin-silver-print), [salted-paper print](#g-salted-paper-print), [archival](#g-archival). [17](#ref-17)

<a id="g-aliasing"></a>
### Aliasing

A false pattern or jagged edge produced when fine detail cannot be represented accurately by the image's sampling grid. **Example:** closely spaced fabric threads become coloured waves. **See also:** [moiré](#g-moire), [optical low-pass filter](#g-optical-low-pass-filter), [pixel](#g-pixel).

<a id="g-alternative-process"></a>
### Alternative process

A photographic method outside the dominant contemporary capture or printing workflow, often involving hand-coated materials or historical chemistry. The category changes with context. **Example:** making a cyanotype from plants placed on sensitised paper. **See also:** [cyanotype](#g-cyanotype), [photogram](#g-photogram), [fine art photography](#g-fine-art-photography).

<a id="g-ambient-light"></a>
### Ambient / available light

Light already present in a scene, including daylight, room lamps or street lighting. Available light can be artificial as well as natural. **Example:** photographing a café using its window and ceiling lights. **See also:** [mixed lighting](#g-mixed-lighting), [continuous light](#g-continuous-light), [fill light](#g-fill-light).

<a id="g-angle-of-view"></a>
### Angle of view / field of view

The extent of a scene recorded by the camera. Angle of view expresses this as an angle; field of view can also mean the width or height covered at a particular distance. **Example:** on the same camera, 24 mm includes more surroundings than 85 mm. **See also:** [focal length](#g-focal-length), [crop factor](#g-crop-factor).

<a id="g-aperture"></a>
### Aperture

The opening through which light passes in a lens, usually adjusted by overlapping diaphragm blades. A wider opening admits more light per unit time and, with other relevant conditions fixed, reduces depth of field. **Example:** change from f/5.6 to f/2.8 to admit four times as much light. **See also:** [f-number](#g-f-number), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Aperture](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#aperture-opening-and-depth-of-field).

<a id="g-aperture-priority"></a>
### Aperture priority (A / Av)

A mode in which you choose aperture and the camera chooses shutter speed; Auto ISO may adjust ISO too. **Example:** set f/8 for a landscape, then check that the chosen shutter speed suits wind-blown foliage. **See also:** [Auto ISO](#g-auto-iso), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation), [shutter priority](#g-shutter-priority).

<a id="g-aps-c"></a>
### APS-C

A family of sensor sizes smaller than full frame, commonly around 24 × 16 mm, with dimensions varying by system. Many have a crop factor near 1.5× or 1.6×. **Example:** a 50 mm lens on a 1.5× body gives approximately the angle of view of 75 mm on full frame. **See also:** [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [equivalent focal length](#g-equivalent-focal-length).

<a id="g-architectural-photography"></a>
### Architectural photography

Photography concerned with buildings, interiors and designed spaces. Viewpoint, light and treatment of perspective help explain or interpret the structure. **Example:** keep a camera level and use lens shift to include a building's upper floors. **See also:** [converging verticals](#g-converging-verticals), [tilt-shift lens](#g-tilt-shift-lens). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Places](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#places-landscape-and-the-built-environment).

<a id="g-archival"></a>
### Archival / preservation

Concerned with keeping photographs identifiable, accessible and physically or digitally usable over time. “Archival” alone does not guarantee a particular lifespan. **Example:** preserve a negative in a suitable enclosure and retain a catalogue identifying its subject. **See also:** [backup](#g-backup), [digital asset management](#g-digital-asset-management), [metadata](#g-metadata). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Photographic circulation and preservation](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#the-public-and-private-lives-of-photographs).

<a id="g-artefact"></a>
### Artefact / artifact

A visible effect introduced by capture, processing or compression rather than by the subject itself. **Example:** blocks appear around lettering in a heavily compressed JPEG. **See also:** [aliasing](#g-aliasing), [compression](#g-compression), [halo](#g-halo).

<a id="g-aspect-ratio"></a>
### Aspect ratio

The relationship between an image's width and height. It describes shape independently of pixel count or print dimensions. **Example:** both a 6000 × 4000-pixel image and a 30 × 20 cm print have a 3:2 ratio. **See also:** [crop](#g-crop), [orientation](#g-orientation), [resolution](#g-resolution).

<a id="g-aspherical-element"></a>
### Aspherical / low-dispersion element

Aspherical lens elements use surfaces that depart from a sphere to help control optical errors. Low-dispersion materials help control colour-related errors. These are different design tools. **Example:** a lens combines both to improve detail across the frame. **See also:** [aberration](#g-aberration), [chromatic aberration](#g-chromatic-aberration). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Lens construction](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#parts-and-features-of-a-lens).

<a id="g-astrophotography"></a>
### Astrophotography

Photography of celestial objects or the night sky. Long exposures and stacking can reveal faint light, while Earth's rotation can turn stars into trails. **Example:** combine several aligned sky exposures to reduce visible noise. **See also:** [long exposure](#g-long-exposure), [stacking](#g-stacking), [noise](#g-noise).

<a id="g-autofocus"></a>
### Autofocus (AF)

A system that automatically adjusts lens focus. Phase-detection and contrast-detection methods, sometimes combined, help determine the required adjustment. **Example:** place the focus area over a portrait subject's eye. **See also:** [continuous autofocus](#g-continuous-autofocus), [single autofocus](#g-single-autofocus), [manual focus](#g-manual-focus). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Focus](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#focus-choosing-what-matters).

<a id="g-auto-iso"></a>
### Auto ISO

A function that adjusts ISO within selected or camera-defined limits. It can operate in manual exposure mode, so choosing aperture and shutter speed does not necessarily fix image brightness. **Example:** hold 1/1000 s and f/4 while ISO changes as a player enters shade. **See also:** [ISO](#g-iso), [manual mode](#g-manual-mode).

## B

<a id="g-back-button-focus"></a>
### Back-button focus

A control arrangement that assigns autofocus activation to a separate button, usually on the camera's rear. It allows focusing and releasing the shutter to be operated independently. **Example:** stop pressing the focus button after focusing on a stationary subject, then reframe. **See also:** [autofocus](#g-autofocus), [focus lock](#g-focus-lock).

<a id="g-background"></a>
### Background / foreground

The parts of a scene behind or in front of the main subject. Their detail, brightness and placement affect attention and the impression of depth. **Example:** include a nearby rock in front of a distant mountain. **See also:** [composition](#g-composition), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [subject separation](#g-subject-separation).

<a id="g-backlighting"></a>
### Backlighting

Lighting that comes from behind the subject relative to the camera. It can emphasise outlines, translucency or silhouettes. **Example:** photograph a leaf with the sun behind it to reveal its veins. **See also:** [rim light](#g-rim-light), [silhouette](#g-silhouette), [flare](#g-flare).

<a id="g-backup"></a>
### Backup

A separate recoverable copy of files. An editing catalogue may contain instructions and previews without containing the original photographs. **Example:** retain originals on a working drive and independent copies on another drive and at another location. **See also:** [catalogue](#g-catalogue), [archival](#g-archival), [workflow](#g-workflow).

<a id="g-balance"></a>
### Balance

The distribution of visual weight within an image. Size, brightness, colour and placement can make a small element visually strong. **Example:** a small bright moon counterbalances a large dark mountain. **See also:** [composition](#g-composition), [negative space](#g-negative-space), [symmetry](#g-symmetry).

<a id="g-banding"></a>
### Banding

Visible stripes or steps that interrupt an image. Tonal banding can arise in smooth gradients; capture bands may result from flickering lights interacting with shutter timing. **Example:** an electronic-shutter photograph under LEDs shows dark horizontal stripes. **See also:** [bit depth](#g-bit-depth), [electronic shutter](#g-electronic-shutter), [posterisation](#g-posterisation).

<a id="g-bayer-pattern"></a>
### Bayer pattern

A common colour-filter arrangement over a sensor, with twice as many green-filtered sites as red or blue. Software reconstructs full-colour pixels from neighbouring measurements. **Example:** a RAW converter reconstructs the colours of fine foliage from the sensor samples. **See also:** [demosaicing](#g-demosaicing), [RAW](#g-raw), [sensor](#g-sensor).

<a id="g-bit-depth"></a>
### Bit depth

The number of binary digits used to represent a sample or channel value. More bits allow more numerical levels, although usable detail also depends on noise and processing. **Example:** an 8-bit RGB channel has 256 possible levels; a 16-bit channel has 65,536. **See also:** [dynamic range](#g-dynamic-range), [posterisation](#g-posterisation), [RAW](#g-raw).

<a id="g-black-and-white"></a>
### Black-and-white / monochrome

An image described through lightness rather than a full range of hues. Monochrome can also include a single-colour tint, such as sepia. **Example:** a black-and-white conversion separates a red flower from green leaves by adjusting their relative tones. **See also:** [tone](#g-tone), [contrast](#g-contrast), [toning](#g-toning).

<a id="g-black-point"></a>
### Black point / white point

In tonal editing, the values mapped to the darkest black and brightest white of the output. In colour management, white point can instead describe the colour of a reference white. **Example:** move a tonal black-point control until a scene's deepest shadow becomes black. **See also:** [clipping](#g-clipping), [histogram](#g-histogram), [colour temperature](#g-colour-temperature).

<a id="g-blue-hour"></a>
### Blue hour

A period of twilight when indirect sky light often gives outdoor scenes a blue appearance. Its duration varies with location, season and conditions. **Example:** balance a blue sky against warm building lights after sunset. **See also:** [golden hour](#g-golden-hour), [colour temperature](#g-colour-temperature), [white balance](#g-white-balance).

<a id="g-blur"></a>
### Blur

Loss or spreading of visible detail through defocus, movement, optics or processing. Identifying the cause determines the useful remedy. **Example:** a person moves during a tripod-mounted exposure, leaving the room sharp and the person blurred. **See also:** [camera shake](#g-camera-shake), [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [focus](#g-focus).

<a id="g-bokeh"></a>
### Bokeh

The visual character of out-of-focus image areas, including how highlights and transitions are rendered. It describes the quality of blur rather than simply its amount. **Example:** compare how two lenses render small lights behind the same focused subject. **See also:** [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [aperture](#g-aperture), [subject separation](#g-subject-separation).

<a id="g-bounce-flash"></a>
### Bounce flash

Flash directed at a surface so reflected light illuminates the subject. The illuminated surface can become a larger apparent source; its colour can tint the light. **Example:** aim a flash at a nearby white wall for softer portrait lighting. **See also:** [soft light](#g-soft-light), [colour cast](#g-colour-cast), [flash](#g-flash).

<a id="g-bracketing"></a>
### Bracketing

Making a sequence with a deliberately varied setting, such as exposure or focus distance. Exposure bracketing and focus bracketing solve different problems. **Example:** capture −2, 0 and +2 EV frames for possible HDR merging. **See also:** [HDR](#g-hdr), [focus stacking](#g-focus-stacking), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation).

<a id="g-brief"></a>
### Brief

A description of an assignment's purpose, audience, required images and delivery conditions. It may also establish the intended uses of the work. **Example:** a restaurant requests horizontal food photographs for a website and vertical versions for posters. **See also:** [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography), [deliverables](#g-deliverables), [licence](#g-licence). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Working life](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#learning-photography-and-building-a-working-life).

<a id="g-buffer"></a>
### Buffer

Temporary memory that holds captured images while they are written to storage. A full buffer can slow continuous shooting. **Example:** a camera begins a fast RAW burst, then drops to a slower rate. **See also:** [burst mode](#g-burst-mode), [memory card](#g-memory-card), [RAW](#g-raw).

<a id="g-bulb"></a>
### Bulb (B)

A shutter mode that allows an exposure to continue while the release is held or a compatible control keeps it open. Operation varies by camera. **Example:** use a remote release for a two-minute night exposure. **See also:** [long exposure](#g-long-exposure), [remote release](#g-remote-release), [tripod](#g-tripod).

<a id="g-burst-mode"></a>
### Burst mode / continuous shooting

Recording successive photographs while the shutter release remains activated. Speed is usually stated in frames per second; focus, format and buffer limits affect performance. **Example:** a burst records several stages of a bird's take-off. **See also:** [buffer](#g-buffer), [continuous autofocus](#g-continuous-autofocus), [frames per second](#g-frames-per-second).

## C

<a id="g-calibration"></a>
### Calibration / profiling

Calibration brings a device towards a defined behaviour; profiling describes its colour response. They are related but distinct operations. **Example:** adjust a monitor to a chosen brightness and white point, then measure it to build a profile. **See also:** [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile), [colour management](#g-colour-management), [soft proofing](#g-soft-proofing).

<a id="g-camera-obscura"></a>
### Camera obscura

A darkened enclosure in which light passing through a small opening or lens projects an inverted image of the outside scene. Projection alone does not preserve the image. **Example:** a pinhole projects a street scene onto the opposite wall of a dark room. **See also:** [pinhole photography](#g-pinhole-photography), [exposure](#g-exposure).

<a id="g-camera-shake"></a>
### Camera shake

Camera movement during an exposure that blurs the projected image. Longer exposures and narrower angles of view often make it more visible. **Example:** a handheld night photograph shows all the streetlights as short streaks. **See also:** [image stabilisation](#g-image-stabilisation), [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [tripod](#g-tripod).

<a id="g-candid"></a>
### Candid photograph

A photograph of an unposed or apparently spontaneous moment. A candid appearance does not establish whether subjects knew about the camera. **Example:** photograph guests talking between arranged wedding groups. **See also:** [street photography](#g-street-photography), [staging](#g-staging), [consent](#g-consent).

<a id="g-caption"></a>
### Caption

Text that identifies or contextualises an image. A factual caption distinguishes verified information from inference. **Example:** identify the people, place and date of a demonstration without guessing what a person's expression means. **See also:** [context](#g-context), [IPTC](#g-iptc), [photojournalism](#g-photojournalism).

<a id="g-catalogue"></a>
### Catalogue / catalog

A database used to organise photographs and, in some applications, record editing instructions. It does not necessarily contain the original image files. **Example:** a catalogue remembers ratings and crops while RAW files remain on an external drive. **See also:** [digital asset management](#g-digital-asset-management), [non-destructive editing](#g-non-destructive-editing), [backup](#g-backup).

<a id="g-catchlight"></a>
### Catchlight

A reflection of a light source visible in an eye. Its shape and position can reveal how the portrait was lit. **Example:** a window creates a rectangular highlight in the subject's pupil area. **See also:** [portrait photography](#g-portrait-photography), [softbox](#g-softbox), [key light](#g-key-light).

<a id="g-chromatic-aberration"></a>
### Chromatic aberration

An optical error caused by wavelengths being focused or magnified differently. It can produce colour fringes across high-contrast edges or before and behind the focus plane. **Example:** purple and green edges appear on branches against bright sky. **See also:** [aberration](#g-aberration), [lens correction](#g-lens-correction).

<a id="g-circle-of-confusion"></a>
### Circle of confusion

The blurred disc formed by a defocused point; in depth-of-field calculations, an assumed maximum acceptable disc size. Acceptability depends on enlargement and viewing conditions. **Example:** a print viewed closely may reveal softness hidden in a small screen preview. **See also:** [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [hyperfocal distance](#g-hyperfocal-distance).

<a id="g-clipping"></a>
### Clipping

Values reaching a recording or output limit so differences are lost. Highlight clipping can remove cloud detail; shadow clipping can turn distinct dark tones into black. **Example:** a bright red flower loses texture when its red channel clips. **See also:** [histogram](#g-histogram), [dynamic range](#g-dynamic-range), [black point](#g-black-point).

<a id="g-close-up"></a>
### Close-up

A photograph made near enough to show a subject or detail prominently. It does not specify a particular reproduction ratio. **Example:** filling the frame with a flower may be close-up work without reaching 1:1 magnification. **See also:** [macro photography](#g-macro-photography), [reproduction ratio](#g-reproduction-ratio), [minimum focusing distance](#g-minimum-focusing-distance).

<a id="g-cmyk"></a>
### CMYK

Cyan, magenta, yellow and black, the process-ink channels used in many printing workflows. A CMYK conversion must suit the intended press conditions. **Example:** use the profile requested by a magazine printer instead of an arbitrary CMYK setting. **See also:** [RGB](#g-rgb), [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile), [gamut](#g-gamut).

<a id="g-colour-cast"></a>
### Colour cast

An overall colour bias, whether unwanted or expressive. It can come from illumination, nearby surfaces, materials or processing. **Example:** a green wall tints a face illuminated by bounced light. **See also:** [white balance](#g-white-balance), [mixed lighting](#g-mixed-lighting), [gel](#g-gel).

<a id="g-colour-management"></a>
### Colour management

A system for interpreting and converting colour values using information about colour spaces and devices. It aims to keep colour predictable across capture, display and output. **Example:** software uses an embedded image profile and a monitor profile to display a photograph. **See also:** [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile), [colour space](#g-colour-space), [rendering intent](#g-rendering-intent).

<a id="g-colour-space"></a>
### Colour space

A defined system for representing colours numerically. The same RGB numbers can represent different colours in different spaces. **Example:** export a web image in sRGB and embed its profile. **See also:** [sRGB](#g-srgb), [Adobe RGB](#g-working-colour-space), [gamut](#g-gamut), [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile).

<a id="g-colour-temperature"></a>
### Colour temperature / correlated colour temperature (CCT)

A description, in kelvin (K), of a light's colour along or near a reference heating sequence. Lower values appear warmer; higher values bluer. It does not describe a lamp's complete spectrum. **Example:** a 3000 K lamp looks warmer than 6500 K daylight illumination. **See also:** [white balance](#g-white-balance), [tint](#g-tint), [mixed lighting](#g-mixed-lighting).

<a id="g-commercial-photography"></a>
### Commercial photography

Photography made to serve a client's business or promotional purpose. It can include products, food, fashion, buildings and people. **Example:** create consistent product views for an online shop. **See also:** [brief](#g-brief), [licence](#g-licence), [still life](#g-still-life). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Evidence, persuasion and invention](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#evidence-persuasion-and-invention).

<a id="g-compact-camera"></a>
### Compact / bridge camera

A compact generally has a permanently attached lens and a portable body. A bridge camera commonly combines a fixed broad-range zoom with more substantial handling. Sensor size varies. **Example:** use a bridge superzoom to cover distant details without changing lenses. **See also:** [mirrorless camera](#g-mirrorless-camera), [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [zoom lens](#g-zoom-lens).

<a id="g-composite"></a>
### Composite / photomontage

An image assembled from multiple source images or parts. Purposes range from seamless technical combination to visible collage. **Example:** combine a separately photographed sky and building into an imagined scene. **See also:** [layer](#g-layer), [mask](#g-mask), [image manipulation](#g-image-manipulation), [panorama](#g-panorama).

<a id="g-composition"></a>
### Composition

The organisation of visual elements within an image. Framing, relationships, light and the instant of capture all contribute. **Example:** move sideways so a lamppost no longer appears to grow from a person's head. **See also:** [balance](#g-balance), [leading lines](#g-leading-lines), [framing](#g-framing), [viewpoint](#g-viewpoint).

<a id="g-compression"></a>
### Compression: files and perspective

File compression reduces storage requirements; lossless methods preserve the encoded data, while lossy methods discard some. Perspective compression is a separate visual description of apparent distance relationships. **Example:** a smaller JPEG may lose fine texture; distant hills may look closely stacked from a far viewpoint. **See also:** [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [lossless](#g-lossless), [perspective compression](#g-perspective-compression).

<a id="g-computational-photography"></a>
### Computational photography

Image-making in which algorithms play a substantial role in constructing the result from captured data. Methods include multi-frame merging and simulated depth effects. **Example:** a phone combines several exposures for a handheld night photograph. **See also:** [HDR](#g-hdr), [portrait mode](#g-portrait-mode), [stacking](#g-stacking).

<a id="g-consent"></a>
### Consent

A person's informed agreement to participation or a proposed use. Agreement to being photographed does not necessarily cover every later context. **Example:** explain the intended exhibition before making a sensitive portrait. **See also:** [model release](#g-model-release), [context](#g-context), [documentary photography](#g-documentary-photography).

<a id="g-contact-sheet"></a>
### Contact sheet / contact print

A contact print is made with a negative directly against photographic paper. A contact sheet presents a set of frames; digital equivalents arrange thumbnails for comparison. **Example:** inspect a whole film roll together to choose the strongest gesture. **See also:** [negative](#g-negative), [culling](#g-culling), [sequence](#g-sequence).

<a id="g-context"></a>
### Context

The circumstances around making and encountering a photograph that affect its interpretation. Captions, sequence and publication setting contribute alongside visible content. **Example:** a cheering crowd means something different when a caption identifies the actual event. **See also:** [caption](#g-caption), [representation](#g-representation), [sequence](#g-sequence). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Public and private lives](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#the-public-and-private-lives-of-photographs).

<a id="g-continuous-autofocus"></a>
### Continuous autofocus (AF-C / Servo)

Autofocus that continues adjusting while activated, often predicting changing subject distance. Tracking capability depends on the camera, lens and settings. **Example:** follow a cyclist approaching the camera. **See also:** [autofocus](#g-autofocus), [subject detection](#g-subject-detection), [burst mode](#g-burst-mode).

<a id="g-continuous-light"></a>
### Continuous light

A source that illuminates throughout viewing and exposure, such as an LED panel or tungsten lamp. Some sources fluctuate rapidly despite appearing steady. **Example:** move an LED panel while watching its shadow change in live view. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [modelling light](#g-modelling-light), [banding](#g-banding).

<a id="g-contrast"></a>
### Contrast

The degree of difference between tones or colours. Overall contrast concerns the image's broad range; local contrast concerns differences between nearby areas. **Example:** side lighting makes a stone wall's texture more pronounced. **See also:** [tone](#g-tone), [dynamic range](#g-dynamic-range), [hard light](#g-hard-light).

<a id="g-converging-verticals"></a>
### Converging verticals / keystone effect

The appearance of parallel upright lines meeting towards the top or bottom when the camera is tilted relative to them. This is perspective, distinct from curved-line lens distortion. **Example:** a building narrows upwards when photographed with the camera tilted up. **See also:** [perspective](#g-perspective), [distortion](#g-distortion), [tilt-shift lens](#g-tilt-shift-lens).

<a id="g-copyright"></a>
### Copyright

Legal rights governing specified uses of creative work, including photographs. Ownership, exceptions and duration depend on applicable law and circumstances. **Example:** a picture credit identifies a creator but does not itself grant permission to reproduce the photograph. **See also:** [licence](#g-licence), [credit](#g-credit), [public domain](#g-public-domain).

<a id="g-credit"></a>
### Credit / byline

An attribution naming the photographer or another relevant source or rights holder. It is distinct from permission to use the work. **Example:** an exhibition label names the photographer and supplying collection. **See also:** [copyright](#g-copyright), [caption](#g-caption), [IPTC](#g-iptc).

<a id="g-crop"></a>
### Crop / cropping

Selecting a smaller part of an image or recording area. Cropping changes framing and usually reduces the available pixel count. **Example:** crop a horizontal photograph into a square to remove a distracting edge. **See also:** [aspect ratio](#g-aspect-ratio), [crop factor](#g-crop-factor), [resolution](#g-resolution).

<a id="g-crop-factor"></a>
### Crop factor

The ratio between a reference format's diagonal and another format's diagonal, commonly using full frame as the reference. It helps compare angles of view without changing a lens's physical focal length. **Example:** 35 mm × 1.6 gives a 56 mm full-frame-equivalent view. **See also:** [equivalent focal length](#g-equivalent-focal-length), [APS-C](#g-aps-c). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Focal length and lens types](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#focal-length-and-lens-types).

<a id="g-culling"></a>
### Culling / picture selection

Reviewing a set of photographs to decide which to keep, develop or publish. It involves judging expression, meaning and relationships as well as technical quality. **Example:** choose one expressive frame from a burst of nearly identical portraits. **See also:** [editing](#g-editing), [contact sheet](#g-contact-sheet), [sequence](#g-sequence).

<a id="g-curves"></a>
### Curves / tone curve

An editing control that maps input tonal values to output values. Individual colour-channel curves can also change colour balance. **Example:** a gentle S-shaped curve deepens darker tones and brightens lighter ones. **See also:** [contrast](#g-contrast), [histogram](#g-histogram), [black point](#g-black-point).

<a id="g-cyanotype"></a>
### Cyanotype

An iron-based photographic process producing a Prussian-blue image, commonly by contact exposure. **Example:** expose leaves placed on sensitised paper, then wash the print to reveal pale plant shapes against blue. **See also:** [alternative process](#g-alternative-process), [photogram](#g-photogram), [contact sheet](#g-contact-sheet).

## D

<a id="g-daguerreotype"></a>
### Daguerreotype

An early process producing a unique image on a silver-coated metal plate. Its reflective surface changes appearance with viewing angle. **Example:** a small cased portrait has no separate negative from which ordinary duplicate prints were made. **See also:** [negative](#g-negative), [wet-plate collodion](#g-wet-plate-collodion), [archival](#g-archival).

<a id="g-darkroom"></a>
### Darkroom

A space arranged for handling and processing light-sensitive photographic materials under appropriate lighting conditions. Many films require total darkness during loading; some papers permit a suitable safelight. **Example:** project a negative onto paper with an enlarger. **See also:** [development](#g-development), [enlargement](#g-enlargement), [safelight](#g-safelight).

<a id="g-decisive-moment"></a>
### Decisive moment

An idea associated with Henri Cartier-Bresson: recognising an instant when an event's significance and visual arrangement come together. It is an approach to timing rather than a camera function. **Example:** release the shutter when a moving figure completes a geometric composition. **See also:** [street photography](#g-street-photography), [composition](#g-composition). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Influential photographers](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#photographers-who-changed-what-a-photograph-could-be).

<a id="g-deliverables"></a>
### Deliverables

The agreed images and accompanying materials supplied for an assignment. These may specify quantity, format, dimensions, colour space, captions and deadline. **Example:** deliver twelve edited TIFFs plus smaller captioned JPEGs. **See also:** [brief](#g-brief), [export](#g-export), [licence](#g-licence).

<a id="g-demosaicing"></a>
### Demosaicing

Reconstructing full-colour pixels from a sensor's differently colour-filtered samples. Algorithms can differ in their handling of detail and false colour. **Example:** fine fabric looks different when processed by two RAW converters. **See also:** [Bayer pattern](#g-bayer-pattern), [moiré](#g-moire), [RAW](#g-raw).

<a id="g-density"></a>
### Density / optical density

A logarithmic measure of how strongly a material reduces transmitted or reflected light. Film, filters and prints have densities that can be measured. **Example:** a denser negative area passes less enlarger light and normally prints lighter. **See also:** [negative](#g-negative), [neutral-density filter](#g-neutral-density-filter), [sensitometry](#g-sensitometry).

<a id="g-depth-of-field"></a>
### Depth of field (DoF)

The range of subject distances that appears acceptably sharp under stated viewing conditions. Aperture, magnification, focus distance and viewing criteria all matter; its boundaries are gradual. **Example:** a group portrait at f/8 may keep more rows acceptably sharp than f/2.8. **See also:** [circle of confusion](#g-circle-of-confusion), [hyperfocal distance](#g-hyperfocal-distance). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Aperture](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#aperture-opening-and-depth-of-field).

<a id="g-development"></a>
### Development / developer

Chemical processing that makes a film or paper's latent image visible. In digital software, “developing” refers instead to interpreting capture data and making adjustments. **Example:** process exposed black-and-white film in developer before stopping and fixing it. **See also:** [latent image](#g-latent-image), [fixer](#g-fixer), [RAW](#g-raw).

<a id="g-diffraction"></a>
### Diffraction

The spreading of light associated with its wave behaviour. In photography, small apertures broaden the image of a point and can reduce fine detail even at accurate focus. **Example:** f/22 gives more depth of field but less crisp fine texture than f/8. **See also:** [aperture](#g-aperture), [sharpness](#g-sharpness), [focus stacking](#g-focus-stacking).

<a id="g-diffuser"></a>
### Diffuser

A translucent material that scatters transmitted light. It softens shadows when it creates a sufficiently large illuminated source as seen from the subject; scattering alone does not guarantee softness. **Example:** place a large diffusion panel close to a still life. **See also:** [soft light](#g-soft-light), [softbox](#g-softbox), [reflector](#g-reflector).

<a id="g-digital-asset-management"></a>
### Digital asset management (DAM)

Organising, describing, finding, protecting and maintaining digital files. A useful system connects filenames, metadata, storage and backups. **Example:** retrieve all licensed photographs from an assignment using keywords and job identifiers. **See also:** [catalogue](#g-catalogue), [metadata](#g-metadata), [backup](#g-backup).

<a id="g-digital-zoom"></a>
### Digital zoom

Narrowing the displayed view through cropping and processing rather than by optically changing focal length. It may use interpolation or additional computational methods. **Example:** enlarge the centre of a phone image beyond its lens's native view. **See also:** [crop](#g-crop), [interpolation](#g-interpolation), [zoom lens](#g-zoom-lens).

<a id="g-distortion"></a>
### Distortion

In lens terminology, an optical mapping error that bends straight lines, commonly outwards in barrel distortion or inwards in pincushion distortion. Perspective effects are a separate issue. **Example:** a straight horizon bows near the frame edge. **See also:** [lens correction](#g-lens-correction), [perspective](#g-perspective), [fisheye lens](#g-fisheye-lens).

<a id="g-dng"></a>
### DNG (Digital Negative)

An openly documented file format developed by Adobe for photographic data, commonly RAW data. DNG files can hold different kinds of image data and compression. **Example:** a phone records a DNG for later adjustment in compatible software. **See also:** [RAW](#g-raw), [file format](#g-file-format), [lossless](#g-lossless).

<a id="g-documentary-photography"></a>
### Documentary photography

Photography that explores events, conditions or lived experience through an account grounded in the world. Research, captions and editing shape that account. **Example:** document a neighbourhood's changing shops over several years. **See also:** [photojournalism](#g-photojournalism), [context](#g-context), [photo essay](#g-photo-essay). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Evidence, persuasion and invention](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#evidence-persuasion-and-invention).

<a id="g-dodging-and-burning"></a>
### Dodging and burning

Selectively lightening and darkening parts of a photograph. The terms originate in darkroom printing and also describe digital adjustments. **Example:** gently lighten a face and darken a distracting corner. **See also:** [local adjustment](#g-local-adjustment), [mask](#g-mask), [darkroom](#g-darkroom).

<a id="g-dpi"></a>
### DPI (dots per inch)

A measure of output dots, commonly used for printer addressability. It differs from the image's pixels per inch. **Example:** a printer may use many small ink dots to reproduce one image pixel. **See also:** [PPI](#g-ppi), [resolution](#g-resolution), [resampling](#g-resampling).

<a id="g-dslr"></a>
### DSLR / SLR

A single-lens reflex camera uses a mirror to direct the taking lens's view towards an optical viewfinder. A DSLR records digitally; a film SLR records on film. **Example:** the mirror rises during an ordinary DSLR exposure. **See also:** [mirrorless camera](#g-mirrorless-camera), [viewfinder](#g-viewfinder). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Camera types](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#main-types-of-cameras).

<a id="g-dynamic-range"></a>
### Dynamic range

The span between the brightest and darkest levels a scene contains or a system can usefully record or reproduce. Measurement criteria matter. **Example:** a bright window exceeds what a single exposure can retain alongside a dark interior. **See also:** [HDR](#g-hdr), [clipping](#g-clipping), [exposure latitude](#g-exposure-latitude).

## E

<a id="g-editing"></a>
### Editing

Either selecting and arranging photographs or changing an image's appearance. Naming the task avoids confusion between picture editing and pixel adjustment. **Example:** edit a hundred frames into a ten-picture story, then adjust those ten files. **See also:** [culling](#g-culling), [sequence](#g-sequence), [post-processing](#g-post-processing).

<a id="g-editorial-photography"></a>
### Editorial photography

Photography made or used to inform or illustrate editorial content. It can include reporting, portraits and commissioned illustrations; “editorial” does not automatically mean unstaged. **Example:** make an environmental portrait for a magazine profile. **See also:** [photojournalism](#g-photojournalism), [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography), [licence](#g-licence).

<a id="g-electronic-shutter"></a>
### Electronic shutter

Exposure timing controlled through sensor electronics rather than solely by moving shutter curtains. Many sensors read rows at different times, which can produce motion distortion or lighting bands. **Example:** a fast-moving propeller looks bent in silent shooting mode. **See also:** [rolling shutter](#g-rolling-shutter), [global shutter](#g-global-shutter), [mechanical shutter](#g-mechanical-shutter).

<a id="g-emulsion"></a>
### Emulsion / silver halide

A photographic emulsion is a light-sensitive coating, conventionally containing silver-halide crystals dispersed in gelatin. Exposure changes the crystals so development can form an image. **Example:** avoid touching a film's emulsion side while loading it. **See also:** [latent image](#g-latent-image), [development](#g-development), [grain](#g-grain).

<a id="g-enlargement"></a>
### Enlargement

A reproduction larger than the source image. A darkroom enlarger projects a negative onto paper; digital enlargement can mean a larger print or increased pixel dimensions. **Example:** make a 20 × 30 cm print from a 35 mm negative. **See also:** [PPI](#g-ppi), [interpolation](#g-interpolation), [darkroom](#g-darkroom).

<a id="g-environmental-portrait"></a>
### Environmental portrait

A portrait in which surroundings contribute to the account of the person. It can be directed or observational. **Example:** photograph a craftsperson at a workbench with tools visible. **See also:** [portrait photography](#g-portrait-photography), [context](#g-context), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — People](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#people-encounter-observation-and-performance).

<a id="g-equivalent-focal-length"></a>
### Equivalent focal length

A focal-length comparison describing a similar angle of view on another format, usually full frame. It does not alone establish equivalent depth of field, noise or exposure settings. **Example:** 25 mm on Micro Four Thirds gives approximately a 50 mm full-frame-equivalent view. **See also:** [crop factor](#g-crop-factor), [focal length](#g-focal-length).

<a id="g-ev"></a>
### EV (exposure value)

A logarithmic scale for combinations of aperture and exposure time. At a specified ISO it can also describe scene-light levels; compensation controls use EV increments, with 1 EV corresponding to one stop. **Example:** +1 EV compensation requests one stop more exposure or brightness through the available automatic control. **See also:** [stop](#g-stop), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation).

<a id="g-exif"></a>
### Exif (Exchangeable image file format)

A standard defining image-file structures and metadata commonly recording capture information such as exposure time, aperture, lens and date. Recorded values depend on equipment and software. **Example:** inspect Exif data to compare the shutter speeds of sharp and blurred frames. **See also:** [metadata](#g-metadata), [IPTC](#g-iptc), [XMP](#g-xmp). [11](#ref-11)

<a id="g-export"></a>
### Export

Creating an output copy with chosen format, dimensions, colour handling and other delivery settings. It commonly renders non-destructive adjustments into the new file. **Example:** export a 2000-pixel-wide sRGB JPEG while retaining the original RAW. **See also:** [deliverables](#g-deliverables), [non-destructive editing](#g-non-destructive-editing), [file format](#g-file-format).

<a id="g-exposure"></a>
### Exposure

The light received per unit area by film or a sensor during capture, determined by illumination at the recording surface and time. The word also informally means one capture or its apparent brightness. **Example:** doubling shutter duration doubles exposure when the light and aperture stay unchanged. **See also:** [ISO](#g-iso), [shutter speed](#g-shutter-speed), [aperture](#g-aperture). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Camera settings](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#camera-settings).

<a id="g-exposure-compensation"></a>
### Exposure compensation

A request for a brighter or darker result than the camera's metering would ordinarily produce. Which setting changes depends on mode, Auto ISO and camera behaviour. **Example:** try +1 EV when a snow scene is rendered too grey. **See also:** [metering](#g-metering), [EV](#g-ev), [manual mode](#g-manual-mode).

<a id="g-exposure-latitude"></a>
### Exposure latitude

The tolerance for changing capture exposure while still obtaining an acceptable result. It depends on the medium, processing and what information must be retained. **Example:** a RAW file may tolerate lifting shadows while already clipped highlights remain unrecoverable. **See also:** [dynamic range](#g-dynamic-range), [clipping](#g-clipping), [RAW](#g-raw).

<a id="g-exposure-lock"></a>
### Exposure lock (AE-L)

Holding the camera's metered exposure decision while recomposing or waiting. It may behave differently when Auto ISO is active. **Example:** meter a face, lock that decision, then include more bright sky in the composition. **See also:** [metering](#g-metering), [focus lock](#g-focus-lock), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation).

<a id="g-extension-tube"></a>
### Extension tube

A hollow spacer between camera and lens that permits closer focusing by moving the lens farther from the recording surface. It generally reduces distant-focus capability and effective light transmission. **Example:** add extension to photograph a small flower more closely. **See also:** [macro photography](#g-macro-photography), [reproduction ratio](#g-reproduction-ratio), [teleconverter](#g-teleconverter).

## F

<a id="g-fashion-photography"></a>
### Fashion / beauty photography

Fashion photography interprets clothing, style and identity; beauty work often concentrates on face, hair, skin or cosmetics. Both can involve substantial collaboration. **Example:** lighting, makeup and pose jointly establish the look of a cosmetics image. **See also:** [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography), [staging](#g-staging), [retouching](#g-retouching).

<a id="g-fast-lens"></a>
### Fast lens

A lens with a relatively wide maximum aperture, allowing a shorter exposure time at a given light level and ISO. “Fast” does not describe autofocus speed. **Example:** f/1.4 admits four times the light of f/2.8, ignoring transmission differences. **See also:** [aperture](#g-aperture), [maximum aperture](#g-maximum-aperture), [shutter speed](#g-shutter-speed).

<a id="g-feathering"></a>
### Feathering

In lighting, aiming a source so the subject receives the edge of its beam. In editing, softening a selection or mask boundary. **Example:** feather a portrait mask so its adjustment fades gradually into the background. **See also:** [mask](#g-mask), [softbox](#g-softbox), [local adjustment](#g-local-adjustment).

<a id="g-file-format"></a>
### File format

The structure used to encode a file's contents. Format affects compatibility, compression, metadata and editing options. **Example:** keep an editable master and supply a JPEG when a publisher requests a ready-to-use image. **See also:** [RAW](#g-raw), [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [TIFF](#g-tiff), [HEIF](#g-heif).

<a id="g-fill-light"></a>
### Fill light / fill flash

Light added to reduce the depth of shadows or balance another source. Fill may come from flash, a lamp or a reflector. **Example:** use gentle flash to brighten a face against a bright sky. **See also:** [key light](#g-key-light), [lighting ratio](#g-lighting-ratio), [reflector](#g-reflector).

<a id="g-film-camera"></a>
### Film / analogue camera

A camera recording images on light-sensitive film rather than a digital sensor. It can use many designs, including SLR, rangefinder, compact and view-camera arrangements. **Example:** a film SLR records a sequence on a 35 mm roll for later processing. **See also:** [roll film](#g-roll-film), [development](#g-development), [DSLR](#g-dslr). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Camera types](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#main-types-of-cameras).

<a id="g-filter"></a>
### Filter

An optical accessory that changes transmitted light, or a software operation that changes image data. These are different stages of image-making. **Example:** a polarising filter reduces a reflection before capture; an editing filter changes the recorded result. **See also:** [polarising filter](#g-polarising-filter), [neutral-density filter](#g-neutral-density-filter), [infrared photography](#g-infrared-photography).

<a id="g-fine-art-photography"></a>
### Fine art / conceptual photography

Fine art photography is made and presented as artistic practice. Conceptual work gives a guiding idea a central role; the approaches can overlap. **Example:** construct a series of staged interiors to investigate memory. **See also:** [genre](#g-genre), [staging](#g-staging), [visualisation](#g-visualisation). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Evidence, persuasion and invention](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#evidence-persuasion-and-invention).

<a id="g-fisheye-lens"></a>
### Fisheye lens

An ultra-wide lens using a projection that deliberately curves most straight lines away from the image centre. Some produce a circular image; others fill the frame. **Example:** exaggerate the curve of a skatepark bowl. **See also:** [wide-angle lens](#g-wide-angle-lens), [distortion](#g-distortion), [angle of view](#g-angle-of-view).

<a id="g-fixer"></a>
### Fixer / stop bath

In conventional black-and-white processing, stop bath halts development and fixer removes undeveloped light-sensitive silver halides. Suitable washing follows to remove processing residues. **Example:** a developed paper print is stopped, fixed and washed before normal viewing. **See also:** [development](#g-development), [darkroom](#g-darkroom), [gelatin-silver print](#g-gelatin-silver-print).

<a id="g-flag"></a>
### Flag / gobo

A device used to block or shape light. In photographic studios, “gobo” may mean an intervening object; in projection lighting it often means a patterned template. **Example:** place black card beside a bottle to create a defined dark reflection. **See also:** [negative fill](#g-negative-fill), [reflector](#g-reflector), [still life](#g-still-life).

<a id="g-flare"></a>
### Flare / ghosting

Unwanted stray light within an optical system that lowers contrast or creates visible shapes. Ghosting can also describe misaligned moving details in a multi-frame merge. **Example:** the sun just outside the frame produces coloured spots and a washed-out area. **See also:** [lens hood](#g-lens-hood), [HDR](#g-hdr), [contrast](#g-contrast).

<a id="g-flash"></a>
### Flash / speedlight / strobe

A source producing a brief burst of light. “Speedlight” commonly means a portable hot-shoe flash; “strobe” often means a studio flash. **Example:** combine flash on a person with ambient light on the background. **See also:** [flash duration](#g-flash-duration), [TTL](#g-ttl), [flash synchronisation](#g-flash-synchronisation).

<a id="g-flash-duration"></a>
### Flash duration

The time over which a flash emits its pulse. Figures measured at t.1 and t.5 thresholds are not interchangeable. Short duration can freeze movement when flash dominates the recorded light. **Example:** capture a crisp splash using a brief flash pulse in a dim room. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [high-speed sync](#g-high-speed-sync). [15](#ref-15)

<a id="g-flash-exposure-compensation"></a>
### Flash exposure compensation (FEC)

A control requesting more or less automatic flash output. Its interaction with general exposure compensation varies by system. **Example:** set −1 FEC to make TTL fill flash subtler while retaining the chosen ambient exposure. **See also:** [TTL](#g-ttl), [fill light](#g-fill-light), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation).

<a id="g-flash-synchronisation"></a>
### Flash synchronisation / X-sync

Timing the flash so its light reaches the intended frame area. A focal-plane shutter's normal maximum sync speed is generally the fastest time when the whole frame can receive one ordinary flash pulse. **Example:** exceeding normal sync without a supported mode produces a dark band. **See also:** [high-speed sync](#g-high-speed-sync), [rear-curtain sync](#g-rear-curtain-sync), [mechanical shutter](#g-mechanical-shutter).

<a id="g-flat-lay"></a>
### Flat lay

An arrangement photographed from above, usually with the camera facing a flat surface. Relationships between objects, spacing and shadows establish the composition. **Example:** arrange ingredients on a table for a recipe illustration. **See also:** [still life](#g-still-life), [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography), [composition](#g-composition).

<a id="g-f-number"></a>
### F-number / f-stop

The ratio of focal length to entrance-pupil diameter, written f/2.8, f/4 and so on. Lower numbers mean wider relative openings; each full stop changes light by a factor of two. **Example:** a 50 mm lens at f/2 has an entrance pupil approximately 25 mm across. **See also:** [aperture](#g-aperture), [stop](#g-stop), [T-stop](#g-t-stop).

<a id="g-focal-length"></a>
### Focal length

An optical property describing a lens's image-forming scale, usually stated in millimetres for focus at infinity. It is not simply lens-barrel length. On a fixed format, longer focal lengths give narrower views. **Example:** change from 24 mm to 100 mm to frame a distant detail more tightly. **See also:** [angle of view](#g-angle-of-view), [equivalent focal length](#g-equivalent-focal-length). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Lenses](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#lenses).

<a id="g-focus"></a>
### Focus / plane of focus

Focus places the sharpest rendering at a chosen subject distance. The plane of focus is the idealised surface of best focus; real lenses may show field curvature. **Example:** focus on the nearer eye in a close portrait. **See also:** [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [autofocus](#g-autofocus), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-focus-breathing"></a>
### Focus breathing

A change in framing or image magnification as focus distance changes. It can complicate focus stacks and visible focus transitions. **Example:** the background appears to grow slightly while focus moves from near to far. **See also:** [focus stacking](#g-focus-stacking), [focal length](#g-focal-length).

<a id="g-focus-lock"></a>
### Focus lock

Holding a focus setting after it has been acquired. Reframing can still move the intended subject outside shallow depth of field. **Example:** lock focus on a stationary object, then wait for someone to enter that distance. **See also:** [single autofocus](#g-single-autofocus), [back-button focus](#g-back-button-focus), [zone focusing](#g-zone-focusing).

<a id="g-focus-peaking"></a>
### Focus peaking

A display aid that highlights edges with strong local contrast to assist manual focusing. It is an indication rather than proof of exact focus. **Example:** use peaking to approach focus, then magnify the eye for confirmation. **See also:** [manual focus](#g-manual-focus), [live view](#g-live-view), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-focus-stacking"></a>
### Focus stacking

Combining frames focused at different distances to create an image with a wider sharp region. Movement and changes in magnification can cause merging errors. **Example:** merge successive focus positions through a stationary flower. **See also:** [bracketing](#g-bracketing), [macro photography](#g-macro-photography), [stacking](#g-stacking). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Movement and scale](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#movement-and-scale-sport-wildlife-and-close-up-work).

<a id="g-format"></a>
### Format

Depending on context, a recording-area size, film type, image shape or file structure. Specify which meaning is intended. **Example:** “medium format” concerns recording size; “TIFF format” concerns file encoding. **See also:** [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [file format](#g-file-format), [aspect ratio](#g-aspect-ratio).

<a id="g-frames-per-second"></a>
### Frames per second (fps)

The number of images recorded or displayed each second. In still photography it commonly describes burst speed; in video it describes recording or playback rate. **Example:** a 10 fps burst captures approximately ten still frames in one second if maintained. **See also:** [burst mode](#g-burst-mode), [buffer](#g-buffer), [time-lapse](#g-time-lapse).

<a id="g-framing"></a>
### Framing

Choosing what lies inside the picture boundary and how it relates to the edges. The term also describes using elements within a scene as a visual frame. **Example:** photograph a person through a doorway. **See also:** [composition](#g-composition), [crop](#g-crop), [viewpoint](#g-viewpoint).

<a id="g-full-frame"></a>
### Full frame

A digital format approximately matching a 35 mm still-film frame, about 36 × 24 mm. It is a size reference rather than a guarantee of quality. **Example:** compare a lens's full-frame view with its narrower view on APS-C. **See also:** [APS-C](#g-aps-c), [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [medium format](#g-medium-format).

## G

<a id="g-gamut"></a>
### Gamut

The range of colours a colour space or device can represent or reproduce under specified conditions. **Example:** a vivid screen green may fall outside a paper-and-printer combination's gamut. **See also:** [colour space](#g-colour-space), [rendering intent](#g-rendering-intent), [soft proofing](#g-soft-proofing).

<a id="g-gel"></a>
### Gel

A coloured filter placed over a light to change its colour. It may create an effect or help match another source. **Example:** warm a daylight-balanced flash to resemble tungsten room lighting. **See also:** [mixed lighting](#g-mixed-lighting), [colour temperature](#g-colour-temperature), [white balance](#g-white-balance).

<a id="g-gelatin-silver-print"></a>
### Gelatin-silver print

A photographic print whose image consists of silver particles in a gelatin layer, commonly made from a negative on black-and-white paper. **Example:** enlarge a film portrait onto conventional fibre-based photographic paper. **See also:** [darkroom](#g-darkroom), [negative](#g-negative), [toning](#g-toning).

<a id="g-genre"></a>
### Genre

A grouping based on subject, purpose, approach or convention. Genres overlap and change with use. **Example:** a portrait of an athlete at training can belong to portraiture, sport and documentary work. **See also:** [documentary photography](#g-documentary-photography), [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography), [fine art photography](#g-fine-art-photography). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Subjects and approaches](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#subjects-approaches-and-photographic-choices).

<a id="g-global-shutter"></a>
### Global shutter

A sensor shutter design that begins and ends light integration across the image area simultaneously. It avoids the row-timing geometry of rolling capture, although other limits remain. **Example:** a rapidly rotating blade retains a more accurate shape. **See also:** [rolling shutter](#g-rolling-shutter), [electronic shutter](#g-electronic-shutter).

<a id="g-golden-hour"></a>
### Golden hour

The period around low sun when direct light is often warmer and shadows longer. Weather and geography affect both the appearance and duration. **Example:** use low evening sunlight to reveal ridges in a landscape. **See also:** [blue hour](#g-blue-hour), [side lighting](#g-side-lighting), [colour temperature](#g-colour-temperature).

<a id="g-graduated-nd-filter"></a>
### Graduated neutral-density filter (GND)

A filter that transitions between darker and clearer areas to reduce brightness selectively before capture. **Example:** position the darker part over a bright sky while leaving land less affected. **See also:** [neutral-density filter](#g-neutral-density-filter), [bracketing](#g-bracketing), [HDR](#g-hdr).

<a id="g-grain"></a>
### Grain / graininess

The visible texture associated with a photographic emulsion's image structure. Its appearance depends on film, exposure, development and enlargement. Digital grain effects imitate an appearance. **Example:** grain becomes more obvious when enlarging a small negative. **See also:** [noise](#g-noise), [push processing](#g-push-processing), [enlargement](#g-enlargement).

<a id="g-grey-card"></a>
### Grey card / neutral reference

A reference with a known neutral appearance, used for exposure or colour checks. A mid-grey exposure card and a spectrally neutral white-balance target need not be interchangeable. **Example:** photograph a neutral target under the subject's lighting for later white-balance adjustment. **See also:** [white balance](#g-white-balance), [metering](#g-metering), [calibration](#g-calibration).

<a id="g-grid"></a>
### Grid / snoot

Modifiers that limit the spread of a light. A grid channels it through cells; a snoot uses a narrowing tube or cone. Neither automatically softens it. **Example:** light a person's hair while limiting spill onto the background. **See also:** [rim light](#g-rim-light), [flag](#g-flag), [hard light](#g-hard-light).

<a id="g-guide-number"></a>
### Guide number (GN)

A flash-output rating relating aperture and distance at a specified ISO, beam coverage and unit of distance. For direct flash under matching conditions, GN is approximately f-number × distance. **Example:** GN 40 in metres suggests f/8 at 5 m at the rated ISO and zoom setting. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [inverse-square law](#g-inverse-square-law), [TTL](#g-ttl).

## H

<a id="g-halo"></a>
### Halo

A light or dark outline around an edge, often caused by excessive sharpening, local contrast or merging. **Example:** a pale border appears around mountains after strong sharpening. **See also:** [sharpening](#g-sharpening), [HDR](#g-hdr), [artefact](#g-artefact).

<a id="g-hard-light"></a>
### Hard light

Light producing relatively abrupt shadow edges, usually from a source small in apparent size from the subject. Hardness and overall contrast are different properties. **Example:** direct sun gives a crisp nose shadow even when a reflector brightens it. **See also:** [soft light](#g-soft-light), [fill light](#g-fill-light), [contrast](#g-contrast).

<a id="g-hdr"></a>
### HDR (high dynamic range)

Capture, processing or display methods handling a wider brightness range. Exposure merging, tone mapping and HDR display output are related but distinct stages. **Example:** merge bracketed interior photographs to retain window and room detail. **See also:** [bracketing](#g-bracketing), [tone mapping](#g-tone-mapping), [dynamic range](#g-dynamic-range).

<a id="g-heif"></a>
### HEIF / HEIC

HEIF is a container format for images and associated data. HEIC commonly identifies HEIF files using HEVC compression. Supported bit depth and features depend on the file and application. **Example:** convert a phone's HEIC to a compatible delivery format when requested. **See also:** [file format](#g-file-format), [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [export](#g-export).

<a id="g-high-key"></a>
### High key / low key

High-key images are dominated by light tones; low-key images by dark tones. Neither requires accidental overexposure or underexposure. **Example:** a white-clothed sitter against a pale background makes a high-key portrait with retained fabric detail. **See also:** [tone](#g-tone), [clipping](#g-clipping), [exposure](#g-exposure).

<a id="g-highlights"></a>
### Highlights / shadows / midtones

The brighter, darker and intermediate tonal regions of an image. Their boundaries are descriptive rather than fixed across every scene. **Example:** a cloud contains highlights, a shaded doorway shadows and a face midtones. **See also:** [histogram](#g-histogram), [tone](#g-tone), [clipping](#g-clipping).

<a id="g-high-speed-sync"></a>
### High-speed sync (HSS / FP sync)

A compatible flash mode that illuminates a frame during shutter travel at speeds above normal focal-plane sync, commonly through rapid pulses. Available output and reach are reduced. **Example:** add flash to an outdoor f/1.8 portrait at 1/2000 s. **See also:** [flash synchronisation](#g-flash-synchronisation), [flash duration](#g-flash-duration), [neutral-density filter](#g-neutral-density-filter). [6](#ref-6)

<a id="g-histogram"></a>
### Histogram

A graph showing how image values are distributed, commonly with dark values left and bright values right. Camera histograms generally describe a processed preview rather than untouched RAW data. **Example:** inspect the individual RGB channels when a saturated flower appears to lose detail. **See also:** [clipping](#g-clipping), [RAW](#g-raw), [highlights](#g-highlights).

<a id="g-hot-shoe"></a>
### Hot shoe

A mounting point with electrical contacts for a flash or compatible accessory. Physical fit alone does not ensure full electronic compatibility. **Example:** a third-party flash may fire manually while lacking TTL support on a particular camera. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [TTL](#g-ttl), [radio trigger](#g-radio-trigger).

<a id="g-hue"></a>
### Hue

The colour attribute described by names such as red, green or blue, distinct from lightness and saturation. **Example:** change a yellow-orange leaf towards red while retaining its brightness. **See also:** [saturation](#g-saturation), [luminance](#g-luminance), [colour space](#g-colour-space).

<a id="g-hyperfocal-distance"></a>
### Hyperfocal distance

For chosen lens, aperture and sharpness criteria, the nearest focus distance that includes infinity within acceptable depth of field. The near limit is approximately half that distance in common calculations. **Example:** use it as a landscape starting point, then inspect foreground and distant detail. **See also:** [circle of confusion](#g-circle-of-confusion), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [zone focusing](#g-zone-focusing). [3](#ref-3)

## I

<a id="g-icc-profile"></a>
### ICC profile

A standardised description used to interpret a colour space or device's colour behaviour. Assigning a profile changes interpretation; converting profiles changes values to preserve appearance as far as possible. **Example:** convert Adobe RGB to sRGB for delivery rather than merely relabelling it. **See also:** [colour management](#g-colour-management), [calibration](#g-calibration), [colour space](#g-colour-space). [7](#ref-7)

<a id="g-image-circle"></a>
### Image circle

The circular area a lens projects with usable illumination and image quality. It must cover the recording area and any required lens movements. **Example:** shifting a lens too far can reveal dark corners beyond its usable coverage. **See also:** [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [vignetting](#g-vignetting), [tilt-shift lens](#g-tilt-shift-lens).

<a id="g-image-manipulation"></a>
### Image manipulation

Changing a photograph's content or appearance. What alterations are acceptable depends on its purpose and the expectations of its audience. **Example:** removing an object may suit an advertisement but misrepresent a reported event. **See also:** [retouching](#g-retouching), [composite](#g-composite), [photojournalism](#g-photojournalism). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Evidence, persuasion and invention](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#evidence-persuasion-and-invention).

<a id="g-image-stabilisation"></a>
### Image stabilisation (IS / VR / IBIS / OIS)

Methods that reduce blur caused by camera movement. Optical systems move lens elements, IBIS moves the sensor, and digital methods process or reposition image data. **Example:** stabilisation helps a handheld photograph of a stationary building but does not stop a walking person. **See also:** [camera shake](#g-camera-shake), [motion blur](#g-motion-blur). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Specifications](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#specifications-that-matter).

<a id="g-incident-metering"></a>
### Incident metering

Measuring light arriving at the subject, usually with a handheld meter. Unlike reflected readings, it is not directly changed by the subject's surface reflectance. **Example:** meter at a sitter's position to establish a studio exposure. **See also:** [reflected metering](#g-reflected-metering), [metering](#g-metering), [lighting ratio](#g-lighting-ratio).

<a id="g-infrared-photography"></a>
### Infrared photography

Recording infrared radiation, usually near-infrared reflected light in creative photography, using suitable film or a compatible camera and filters. It is distinct from thermal imaging. **Example:** foliage appears unusually bright while a clear sky becomes dark. **See also:** [filter](#g-filter), [black-and-white](#g-black-and-white), [sensor](#g-sensor).

<a id="g-instant-photography"></a>
### Instant photography

Photography using materials that produce a physical photograph shortly after exposure through an integrated chemical process. It differs from a digital camera connected to a separate printer. **Example:** an instant camera ejects a sheet that develops into a visible print. **See also:** [film camera](#g-film-camera), [development](#g-development), [positive](#g-negative).

<a id="g-interpolation"></a>
### Interpolation

Estimating new values from existing samples, commonly when changing pixel dimensions or transforming an image. Conventional interpolation cannot restore detail that was never recorded. **Example:** enlarge a 3000-pixel-wide photograph to 6000 pixels for a particular output workflow. **See also:** [resampling](#g-resampling), [digital zoom](#g-digital-zoom), [resolution](#g-resolution).

<a id="g-inverse-square-law"></a>
### Inverse-square law

For an ideal point source, illumination falls with the square of distance: twice as far receives one-quarter as much. Large nearby sources and reflected environments depart from this simple model. **Example:** moving a small flash from 1 m to 2 m reduces illumination by about two stops. **See also:** [stop](#g-stop), [guide number](#g-guide-number), [lighting ratio](#g-lighting-ratio).

<a id="g-iptc"></a>
### IPTC photo metadata

Standardised properties describing photographs, including captions, creators, subjects and rights information. They help people and systems exchange understandable image records. **Example:** embed a verified caption and creator credit before supplying images to an editor. **See also:** [metadata](#g-metadata), [Exif](#g-exif), [XMP](#g-xmp), [keyword](#g-keyword). [10](#ref-10)

<a id="g-iso"></a>
### ISO / ASA / DIN

ISO expresses film speed and, in digital photography, exposure-index settings associated with signal handling and rendering. Raising digital ISO does not make the sensor collect more photons. ASA and DIN appear on older film equipment. **Example:** ISO 1600 supports a faster metered shutter than ISO 400 at the same aperture. **See also:** [exposure](#g-exposure), [noise](#g-noise), [Auto ISO](#g-auto-iso). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — ISO](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#iso-brightness-and-image-quality).

## J

<a id="g-jpeg"></a>
### JPEG / JPG

A widely supported format commonly using lossy compression for processed photographs. Repeated editing and resaving can accumulate losses. A JPEG can still be carefully edited within its limits. **Example:** export a good-quality JPEG for a website from a retained master. **See also:** [RAW](#g-raw), [compression](#g-compression), [export](#g-export).

## K

<a id="g-key-light"></a>
### Key light / main light

The principal light shaping the subject's appearance. It need not be the only source or the light nearest the camera. **Example:** use a window as the key and white card as fill. **See also:** [fill light](#g-fill-light), [lighting ratio](#g-lighting-ratio), [side lighting](#g-side-lighting).

<a id="g-keyword"></a>
### Keyword

A descriptive term attached to a photograph to support searching and organisation. Consistent names and spellings make a collection easier to find. **Example:** apply both a location name and a subject term to photographs from a project. **See also:** [metadata](#g-metadata), [IPTC](#g-iptc), [digital asset management](#g-digital-asset-management).

<a id="g-kit-lens"></a>
### Kit lens

A lens sold with a camera as a package, often a general-purpose zoom. The label describes how it is supplied rather than a fixed level of quality. **Example:** use an 18–55 mm kit zoom to learn which angles of view suit your work. **See also:** [zoom lens](#g-zoom-lens), [maximum aperture](#g-maximum-aperture). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Getting started](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#getting-started).

## L

<a id="g-landscape-photography"></a>
### Landscape photography

Photography interpreting places and their spatial, environmental or cultural relationships. It can include wilderness, cities and human alteration. **Example:** use repeated viewpoints to document a valley as construction changes it. **See also:** [aerial photography](#g-aerial-photography), [architectural photography](#g-architectural-photography), [viewpoint](#g-viewpoint). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Places](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#places-landscape-and-the-built-environment).

<a id="g-large-format"></a>
### Large format / view camera

Large-format photography commonly uses individual sheets of film, such as 4 × 5 inches or larger. View cameras often allow independent lens and recording-plane movements. **Example:** tilt the lens plane to align focus along a tabletop. **See also:** [medium format](#g-medium-format), [tilt-shift lens](#g-tilt-shift-lens), [format](#g-format).

<a id="g-latent-image"></a>
### Latent image

The invisible changes formed in a photographic emulsion by exposure, which development turns into a visible image. **Example:** an exposed film roll contains latent images before processing. **See also:** [development](#g-development), [exposure](#g-exposure), [negative](#g-negative).

<a id="g-layer"></a>
### Layer

A separately controllable image element or adjustment in an editing document. Order, opacity and blending affect the combined result. **Example:** place a text layer above a photograph without changing the underlying image pixels. **See also:** [mask](#g-mask), [composite](#g-composite), [non-destructive editing](#g-non-destructive-editing).

<a id="g-leading-lines"></a>
### Leading lines

Visible or implied lines that direct attention through an image. They can lead towards a subject, create movement or carry the eye out of the frame. **Example:** use a river's curve to connect foreground and mountain. **See also:** [composition](#g-composition), [balance](#g-balance), [perspective](#g-perspective).

<a id="g-lens-coating"></a>
### Lens coating

A thin surface treatment used to alter optical behaviour, commonly reducing unwanted reflections. Different treatments can also protect exposed surfaces. **Example:** anti-reflection coatings help preserve contrast when bright lights are near the frame. **See also:** [flare](#g-flare), [contrast](#g-contrast), [lens hood](#g-lens-hood). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Lens construction](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#parts-and-features-of-a-lens).

<a id="g-lens-correction"></a>
### Lens correction / lens profile

Processing that compensates for selected lens characteristics, often distortion, vignetting and lateral chromatic aberration. Corrections may crop or resample the image. **Example:** straighten bowed architectural edges using a matching lens profile. **See also:** [distortion](#g-distortion), [vignetting](#g-vignetting), [chromatic aberration](#g-chromatic-aberration).

<a id="g-lens-hood"></a>
### Lens hood

A shade attached around a lens's front to block some stray light. The hood must suit the lens and recorded angle of view. **Example:** a suitable hood reduces flare from sunlight outside the picture. **See also:** [flare](#g-flare), [vignetting](#g-vignetting), [filter](#g-filter).

<a id="g-lens-mount"></a>
### Lens mount

The mechanical connection, often with electronic contacts, between a lens and camera. Mount fit, image coverage and functional compatibility are separate questions. **Example:** an adapted lens fits but requires manual focus and manual aperture control. **See also:** [image circle](#g-image-circle), [autofocus](#g-autofocus), [sensor size](#g-sensor-size). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Lens parts](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#parts-and-features-of-a-lens).

<a id="g-licence"></a>
### Licence / license

Permission to use a photograph under specified terms, which may define medium, purpose, duration, territory and exclusivity. Permission does not necessarily transfer copyright. **Example:** license a portrait for one magazine issue and its associated website article. **See also:** [copyright](#g-copyright), [rights-managed](#g-rights-managed), [royalty-free](#g-royalty-free), [brief](#g-brief).

<a id="g-lighting-ratio"></a>
### Lighting ratio

A comparison of illumination or exposure between sources or subject areas. State what is compared, since conventions differ. **Example:** the lit cheek measures two stops above the shadow cheek, a 4:1 illumination ratio between those readings. **See also:** [key light](#g-key-light), [fill light](#g-fill-light), [stop](#g-stop).

<a id="g-light-meter"></a>
### Light meter

A device or camera system that measures light to guide exposure decisions. A meter reading requires interpretation in relation to the subject and intended result. **Example:** compare a spot reading from snow with one from a shaded face. **See also:** [metering](#g-metering), [incident metering](#g-incident-metering), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation).

<a id="g-light-painting"></a>
### Light painting

Using a moving light during a long exposure to illuminate selected areas or draw visible trails. **Example:** move a torch over a stationary object while the camera remains on a tripod. **See also:** [long exposure](#g-long-exposure), [bulb](#g-bulb), [tripod](#g-tripod).

<a id="g-live-view"></a>
### Live view

A continuously updated electronic preview from the imaging system. Exposure preview, focus aids and display quality depend on mode and camera. **Example:** magnify live view to check a still-life subject's focus before capture. **See also:** [viewfinder](#g-viewfinder), [manual focus](#g-manual-focus), [mirrorless camera](#g-mirrorless-camera).

<a id="g-local-adjustment"></a>
### Local / global adjustment

A local adjustment affects a selected part of an image; a global adjustment acts across the image according to its control. **Example:** brighten only a face locally after setting overall contrast globally. **See also:** [mask](#g-mask), [dodging and burning](#g-dodging-and-burning), [curves](#g-curves).

<a id="g-long-exposure"></a>
### Long exposure

An exposure long enough for time-related effects to become significant in the situation. There is no universal duration. **Example:** a ten-second exposure turns moving traffic lights into trails. **See also:** [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [neutral-density filter](#g-neutral-density-filter), [reciprocity failure](#g-reciprocity-failure). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Shutter speed](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#shutter-speed-time-and-movement).

<a id="g-lossless"></a>
### Lossless / lossy

Lossless compression permits exact reconstruction of the encoded data; lossy compression does not. Neither label alone describes whether earlier processing altered the original capture. **Example:** losslessly compressing a TIFF preserves its current pixels but cannot restore detail already lost in an earlier JPEG. **See also:** [compression](#g-compression), [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [TIFF](#g-tiff).

<a id="g-luminance"></a>
### Luminance / lightness

Luminance is a measurable light quantity weighted for human visual sensitivity. Lightness describes perceived brightness relative to a reference white; editing tools use related models. **Example:** lighten blue sky in a monochrome conversion without treating all hues identically. **See also:** [hue](#g-hue), [saturation](#g-saturation), [tone](#g-tone).

## M

<a id="g-macro-photography"></a>
### Macro photography

Close-range photography at high reproduction ratios, conventionally around life size on the recording surface, although usage varies. A macro label does not ensure 1:1 capability. **Example:** at 1:1, a 10 mm insect forms a 10 mm image on the sensor. **See also:** [reproduction ratio](#g-reproduction-ratio), [close-up](#g-close-up), [focus stacking](#g-focus-stacking). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Movement and scale](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#movement-and-scale-sport-wildlife-and-close-up-work).

<a id="g-manual-focus"></a>
### Manual focus (MF)

Setting focus yourself using a lens ring or another control. Magnified viewing helps determine whether the intended detail is sharp. **Example:** focus precisely on a stationary small object when autofocus selects its background. **See also:** [focus peaking](#g-focus-peaking), [live view](#g-live-view), [autofocus](#g-autofocus).

<a id="g-manual-mode"></a>
### Manual mode (M)

A mode in which you choose aperture and shutter speed. ISO may remain automatic, depending on the setup. **Example:** set f/8 and 1/125 s for consistent studio exposures with fixed ISO and manual flash. **See also:** [Auto ISO](#g-auto-iso), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation), [program mode](#g-program-mode). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Balancing controls](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#balancing-the-three-controls).

<a id="g-mask"></a>
### Mask

A map controlling where and how strongly a layer or adjustment applies. Selections can use painted areas, brightness, colour, detected objects or other criteria. **Example:** darken the sky while protecting a mountain's edges. **See also:** [layer](#g-layer), [feathering](#g-feathering), [local adjustment](#g-local-adjustment). [12](#ref-12)

<a id="g-maximum-aperture"></a>
### Maximum aperture / variable aperture

Maximum aperture is a lens's widest available opening, expressed by its lowest f-number. On some zooms it becomes smaller towards the long end. **Example:** 18–55 mm f/3.5–5.6 permits f/3.5 at 18 mm and f/5.6 at 55 mm. **See also:** [fast lens](#g-fast-lens), [f-number](#g-f-number), [zoom lens](#g-zoom-lens).

<a id="g-mechanical-shutter"></a>
### Mechanical shutter / leaf shutter

A mechanical shutter controls exposure with moving parts. Focal-plane shutters operate near the recording surface; leaf shutters usually operate within the lens and have different flash-sync behaviour. **Example:** a leaf-shutter lens may support ordinary flash at faster shutter settings than a focal-plane design. **See also:** [electronic shutter](#g-electronic-shutter), [flash synchronisation](#g-flash-synchronisation).

<a id="g-medium-format"></a>
### Medium format

A group of film and digital formats larger than 35 mm/full frame but generally smaller than large-format sheet film. Digital and film dimensions vary considerably. **Example:** a 120-film camera records nominal 6 × 6 cm frames, while another records 6 × 4.5 cm. **See also:** [full frame](#g-full-frame), [large format](#g-large-format), [roll film](#g-roll-film).

<a id="g-megapixel"></a>
### Megapixel (MP)

One million image pixels or sensor photosites, depending on the specification. Pixel count is one contributor to recorded detail and output size. **Example:** 6000 × 4000 pixels gives 24 megapixels. **See also:** [pixel](#g-pixel), [resolution](#g-resolution), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-memory-card"></a>
### Memory card

Removable digital storage used by many cameras. Capacity, physical type and supported sustained write performance affect suitability. **Example:** a camera may slow after its buffer fills even when a card has ample free space. **See also:** [buffer](#g-buffer), [burst mode](#g-burst-mode), [backup](#g-backup).

<a id="g-metadata"></a>
### Metadata

Information describing an image, its capture, authorship, processing or management. It may be embedded in the file or held separately. **Example:** combine capture time, a verified caption and a rights statement in an image record. **See also:** [Exif](#g-exif), [IPTC](#g-iptc), [XMP](#g-xmp), [sidecar file](#g-sidecar-file).

<a id="g-metering"></a>
### Metering / evaluative / centre-weighted / spot

Measuring light to guide exposure. Evaluative or matrix metering considers multiple areas; centre-weighted gives more influence near the centre; spot measures a small area. Implementations vary. **Example:** spot-metering a dark coat alone may make it too light without interpretation. **See also:** [reflected metering](#g-reflected-metering), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation), [grey card](#g-grey-card).

<a id="g-micro-four-thirds"></a>
### Micro Four Thirds (MFT / M43)

A camera system using a sensor format commonly about 17.3 × 13 mm and a dedicated lens mount. Its crop factor is approximately 2× relative to full frame. **Example:** a 25 mm lens gives roughly the full-frame view of 50 mm. **See also:** [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [equivalent focal length](#g-equivalent-focal-length), [lens mount](#g-lens-mount).

<a id="g-minimum-focusing-distance"></a>
### Minimum focusing distance / working distance

Minimum focusing distance is the shortest specified distance from the recording plane to the focused subject. Working distance is the usable space between the lens's front region and subject. **Example:** a lens's 20 cm focusing distance leaves much less than 20 cm for lighting. **See also:** [macro photography](#g-macro-photography), [reproduction ratio](#g-reproduction-ratio).

<a id="g-mirrorless-camera"></a>
### Mirrorless camera

A camera without a reflex mirror, commonly using the imaging sensor for electronic viewing. Interchangeable-lens mirrorless cameras are one category; fixed-lens cameras can also lack mirrors. **Example:** preview white-balance changes in an electronic viewfinder. **See also:** [DSLR](#g-dslr), [viewfinder](#g-viewfinder), [live view](#g-live-view). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Camera types](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#main-types-of-cameras).

<a id="g-mixed-lighting"></a>
### Mixed lighting

Illumination from sources with different colour characteristics. One global white balance may not neutralise all parts of the scene. **Example:** a window-lit face looks cool on one side and warm under a room lamp on the other. **See also:** [white balance](#g-white-balance), [gel](#g-gel), [colour cast](#g-colour-cast).

<a id="g-model-release"></a>
### Model / property release

A document recording permission or agreed terms concerning use of a person's likeness or specified property. Its scope depends on wording and applicable law. **Example:** a commercial assignment includes a release covering an agreed advertising use. **See also:** [consent](#g-consent), [licence](#g-licence), [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography).

<a id="g-modelling-light"></a>
### Modelling light

A continuous lamp associated with a flash that helps preview light direction and shadow placement. It does not necessarily reproduce the flash's brightness, colour or exact distribution. **Example:** position a portrait light using its modelling lamp before checking a flash exposure. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [continuous light](#g-continuous-light).

<a id="g-moire"></a>
### Moiré

A larger false pattern produced when fine repeated detail interacts with another sampling pattern. In digital capture it can appear as waves or false colour. **Example:** a tightly woven jacket develops coloured ripples. **See also:** [aliasing](#g-aliasing), [demosaicing](#g-demosaicing), [optical low-pass filter](#g-optical-low-pass-filter).

<a id="g-motion-blur"></a>
### Motion blur

Blur caused by relative movement during exposure. Subject motion, camera motion and their combination create different patterns. **Example:** a cyclist's wheels blur while a tracked face remains relatively sharp. **See also:** [panning](#g-panning), [camera shake](#g-camera-shake), [shutter speed](#g-shutter-speed).

<a id="g-mtf"></a>
### MTF (modulation transfer function)

A description of how an imaging system transfers contrast at different spatial detail frequencies. Lens charts can be measured or calculated under different conditions, so comparisons need care. **Example:** compare centre and corner curves at the same aperture and test conditions. **See also:** [contrast](#g-contrast), [resolution](#g-resolution), [sharpness](#g-sharpness). [16](#ref-16)

<a id="g-multiple-exposure"></a>
### Multiple exposure

Combining two or more exposures into one frame, in camera or through processing. This may be expressive rather than an attempt to describe one literal instant. **Example:** overlay a portrait with foliage. **See also:** [composite](#g-composite), [long exposure](#g-long-exposure), [staging](#g-staging).

## N

<a id="g-negative"></a>
### Negative / positive / transparency

A negative reverses the scene's tonal relationships; colour negatives also use complementary colour relationships. A positive shows corresponding relationships. A transparency is a positive intended for transmitted-light viewing. **Example:** print a black-and-white negative so dense film areas become light paper tones. **See also:** [development](#g-development), [slide film](#g-slide-film), [contact sheet](#g-contact-sheet).

<a id="g-negative-fill"></a>
### Negative fill

Reducing reflected light reaching a subject, usually with a dark surface, to deepen shadows or define shape. **Example:** place black fabric beside a face in a bright room. **See also:** [fill light](#g-fill-light), [flag](#g-flag), [lighting ratio](#g-lighting-ratio).

<a id="g-negative-space"></a>
### Negative space

Relatively open or quiet image areas around the main subject. It can establish scale, isolation, balance or space for text. **Example:** leave a broad area of sky around a small bird. **See also:** [composition](#g-composition), [balance](#g-balance), [subject separation](#g-subject-separation).

<a id="g-neutral-density-filter"></a>
### Neutral-density filter (ND)

A filter intended to reduce transmitted light without substantially changing colour. Ratings use several systems, so specify stops when comparing. **Example:** a six-stop ND permits about 1 s instead of 1/60 s at the same aperture and ISO. **See also:** [graduated ND filter](#g-graduated-nd-filter), [stop](#g-stop), [long exposure](#g-long-exposure). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Accessories](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#other-accessories).

<a id="g-noise"></a>
### Noise

Unwanted variation in image values, seen as brightness fluctuations or colour speckles. Photon statistics and electronic processes contribute; low captured light often makes noise more visible. **Example:** brightening a severely underexposed shadow reveals mottled colour. **See also:** [signal-to-noise ratio](#g-signal-to-noise-ratio), [ISO](#g-iso), [noise reduction](#g-noise-reduction).

<a id="g-noise-reduction"></a>
### Noise reduction / denoising

Processing that estimates and suppresses noise while trying to retain useful detail. Strong settings may erase texture or introduce misleading structure. **Example:** reduce colour speckles in a night photograph while checking fine hair at full size. **See also:** [noise](#g-noise), [sharpening](#g-sharpening), [stacking](#g-stacking). [12](#ref-12)

<a id="g-non-destructive-editing"></a>
### Non-destructive editing

An approach that retains source data and records adjustments separately or reversibly. The originals and supporting instructions still require preservation. **Example:** change a RAW file's crop in a catalogue, then restore the full frame later. **See also:** [catalogue](#g-catalogue), [sidecar file](#g-sidecar-file), [export](#g-export), [backup](#g-backup).

## O

<a id="g-optical-low-pass-filter"></a>
### Optical low-pass / anti-aliasing filter (OLPF / AA)

A filter in front of some sensors that reduces very fine image detail before sampling to limit aliasing. Designs trade some crispness for fewer false patterns. **Example:** fine textiles may produce less moiré on a filtered sensor. **See also:** [aliasing](#g-aliasing), [moiré](#g-moire), [resolution](#g-resolution).

<a id="g-orientation"></a>
### Orientation: portrait / landscape

Portrait orientation is taller than wide; landscape orientation is wider than tall. The names describe shape rather than subject genre. **Example:** photograph a mountain in vertical portrait orientation to include foreground and sky. **See also:** [aspect ratio](#g-aspect-ratio), [portrait photography](#g-portrait-photography), [landscape photography](#g-landscape-photography).

<a id="g-overexposure"></a>
### Overexposure / underexposure

More or less exposure than the chosen reference or intended result requires. A light or dark-looking picture is not automatically incorrectly exposed. **Example:** extra exposure clips the texture in a white dress that the photographer wanted to preserve. **See also:** [exposure](#g-exposure), [clipping](#g-clipping), [high key](#g-high-key).

## P

<a id="g-panning"></a>
### Panning

Moving the camera to follow a subject during exposure. With a suitable shutter time and accurate movement, the subject remains relatively sharp against a streaked background. **Example:** track a passing cyclist at 1/30 s and compare several attempts. **See also:** [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [sports photography](#g-sports-photography). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Movement and scale](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#movement-and-scale-sport-wildlife-and-close-up-work).

<a id="g-panorama"></a>
### Panorama / stitching

A panoramic image presents an extended view. Stitching joins overlapping frames, often with alignment and blending. Not all wide-shaped photographs are stitched. **Example:** rotate the camera through overlapping views of a valley and merge them. **See also:** [parallax](#g-parallax), [composite](#g-composite), [aspect ratio](#g-aspect-ratio).

<a id="g-parallax"></a>
### Parallax

The apparent displacement of nearby objects relative to distant ones when viewpoint changes. It affects separate viewfinders and stitched panoramas. **Example:** a nearby railing fails to align across panorama frames because the camera rotated around the wrong point. **See also:** [panorama](#g-panorama), [rangefinder](#g-rangefinder), [perspective](#g-perspective).

<a id="g-perspective"></a>
### Perspective

How spatial relationships are projected into an image. Relative apparent sizes depend on viewpoint; changing focal length from one position changes framing rather than those size relationships. **Example:** move close to a face and the nose appears larger relative to the ears. **See also:** [viewpoint](#g-viewpoint), [perspective compression](#g-perspective-compression), [converging verticals](#g-converging-verticals).

<a id="g-perspective-compression"></a>
### Perspective compression

The appearance of reduced spacing between near and distant subjects when viewed from farther away. Long lenses often accompany this viewpoint because they retain tight framing. **Example:** move back and use a longer lens to make distant mountains appear larger behind a person. **See also:** [perspective](#g-perspective), [telephoto lens](#g-telephoto-lens), [focal length](#g-focal-length).

<a id="g-photobook"></a>
### Photobook

A book in which photographs and their arrangement are central to the work. Page turns, image size, paper and text shape the experience. **Example:** alternate wide views and intimate details to create a rhythm across a project. **See also:** [sequence](#g-sequence), [photo essay](#g-photo-essay), [editing](#g-editing). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Circulation](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#the-public-and-private-lives-of-photographs).

<a id="g-photo-essay"></a>
### Photo essay / photographic series

A group of images developed around a subject or idea. A photo essay often builds a narrative or argument; a series can also work through repetition or comparison. **Example:** combine workplace details, portraits and wider scenes to describe a factory. **See also:** [sequence](#g-sequence), [documentary photography](#g-documentary-photography), [photobook](#g-photobook).

<a id="g-photogram"></a>
### Photogram

An image made by placing objects on or near a light-sensitive surface and exposing it, without using a camera lens to form the image. **Example:** translucent glass and opaque leaves create different tones on photographic paper. **See also:** [cyanotype](#g-cyanotype), [alternative process](#g-alternative-process), [darkroom](#g-darkroom).

<a id="g-photojournalism"></a>
### Photojournalism

Photographic reporting for a public audience, grounded in factual accuracy and context. Captions, verification and editorial standards are part of the practice. **Example:** document a news event with verified identities and accurate time and location information. **See also:** [documentary photography](#g-documentary-photography), [caption](#g-caption), [image manipulation](#g-image-manipulation). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Evidence](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#evidence-persuasion-and-invention).

<a id="g-pinhole-photography"></a>
### Pinhole photography

Photography using a tiny opening instead of a lens to form the image. It usually requires long exposures and produces a distinctive softness. **Example:** expose photographic paper inside a light-tight box fitted with a pinhole. **See also:** [camera obscura](#g-camera-obscura), [long exposure](#g-long-exposure), [diffraction](#g-diffraction).

<a id="g-pixel"></a>
### Pixel / photosite

A pixel is an image sample in a digital grid. A photosite is a light-sensing location on a sensor; it need not correspond directly to a full-colour output pixel. **Example:** colour reconstruction combines neighbouring filtered sensor samples to form image pixels. **See also:** [Bayer pattern](#g-bayer-pattern), [demosaicing](#g-demosaicing), [megapixel](#g-megapixel).

<a id="g-platinum-print"></a>
### Platinum / palladium print

A photographic print whose image is formed from platinum, palladium or a mixture of these metals, usually within paper fibres. **Example:** a hand-coated contact print has a matte surface and finely differentiated tones. **See also:** [alternative process](#g-alternative-process), [contact print](#g-contact-sheet), [gelatin-silver print](#g-gelatin-silver-print). [17](#ref-17)

<a id="g-png"></a>
### PNG

A raster-image format supporting lossless compression and transparency. It is useful for graphics but can produce large files for photographs. **Example:** save a diagram with transparent areas as PNG. **See also:** [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [TIFF](#g-tiff), [lossless](#g-lossless).

<a id="g-polarising-filter"></a>
### Polarising filter / circular polariser (CPL)

A filter that selectively transmits polarised light. Rotating it can reduce some non-metallic reflections or darken parts of a blue sky; results depend on angles. **Example:** reduce glare on water to reveal stones beneath it. **See also:** [filter](#g-filter), [neutral-density filter](#g-neutral-density-filter), [angle of view](#g-angle-of-view).

<a id="g-portfolio"></a>
### Portfolio

A selected presentation of work communicating a photographer's abilities, interests or suitability for a purpose. Its strength comes from relevance and coherence as well as individual images. **Example:** show a concise architectural portfolio when approaching a design practice. **See also:** [editing](#g-editing), [sequence](#g-sequence), [brief](#g-brief). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Working life](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#learning-photography-and-building-a-working-life).

<a id="g-portrait-mode"></a>
### Portrait mode

A camera or phone mode optimised for people, often using subject detection, processing or simulated shallow depth of field. Its behaviour varies by device. **Example:** a phone blurs the background computationally but makes errors around loose hair. **See also:** [computational photography](#g-computational-photography), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [portrait photography](#g-portrait-photography).

<a id="g-portrait-photography"></a>
### Portrait photography / portraiture

Photography in which a person or group is central to the image's account. Expression, pose, surroundings and the photographer-subject relationship contribute to meaning. **Example:** a close portrait emphasises expression while an environmental portrait includes a workplace. **See also:** [environmental portrait](#g-environmental-portrait), [consent](#g-consent), [catchlight](#g-catchlight). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — People](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#people-encounter-observation-and-performance).

<a id="g-posterisation"></a>
### Posterisation

Abrupt transitions where smooth tonal or colour changes become visible steps. Limited numerical precision and strong processing can contribute. **Example:** a heavily adjusted sky breaks into bands. **See also:** [bit depth](#g-bit-depth), [banding](#g-banding), [curves](#g-curves).

<a id="g-post-processing"></a>
### Post-processing / postproduction

Work performed after capture to interpret, prepare or change images. It includes RAW conversion, tonal work, retouching and output preparation. **Example:** correct white balance, adjust a crop and export a delivery file. **See also:** [editing](#g-editing), [retouching](#g-retouching), [export](#g-export).

<a id="g-ppi"></a>
### PPI (pixels per inch)

The relationship between pixel dimensions and physical output size. Changing the PPI tag without resampling changes intended print dimensions, not pixel count. **Example:** 3000 pixels printed at 300 ppi spans 10 inches, or 25.4 cm. **See also:** [DPI](#g-dpi), [resampling](#g-resampling), [resolution](#g-resolution).

<a id="g-prime-lens"></a>
### Prime / standard lens

A prime has one focal length. A standard lens gives an angle of view commonly treated as moderate for the format; it can be a prime or part of a zoom's range. **Example:** 50 mm is a conventional full-frame standard prime. **See also:** [zoom lens](#g-zoom-lens), [fast lens](#g-fast-lens), [angle of view](#g-angle-of-view).

<a id="g-program-mode"></a>
### Program mode (P)

A mode in which the camera chooses aperture and shutter speed while often leaving other decisions to the photographer. Program shift, where provided, selects another paired combination. **Example:** shift towards a faster shutter while maintaining approximately the same metered brightness. **See also:** [aperture priority](#g-aperture-priority), [shutter priority](#g-shutter-priority), [manual mode](#g-manual-mode).

<a id="g-public-domain"></a>
### Public domain / Creative Commons

Public-domain status means copyright does not restrict the work in the relevant jurisdiction. Creative Commons licences grant specified permissions under stated conditions. They are different situations. **Example:** a CC BY photograph requires attribution under its licence terms. **See also:** [copyright](#g-copyright), [licence](#g-licence), [credit](#g-credit). [14](#ref-14)

<a id="g-push-processing"></a>
### Push / pull processing

Developing film more or less than a normal process, often to accompany exposure at a different exposure index or alter contrast. Pushing cannot recreate unrecorded shadow information. **Example:** expose ISO 400 film at EI 800 and request an appropriate one-stop push. **See also:** [ISO](#g-iso), [development](#g-development), [grain](#g-grain). [8](#ref-8)

## Q

<a id="g-quality"></a>
### Quality setting

A camera or export setting governing choices such as compression or recorded dimensions. “Highest quality” in a menu does not guarantee accurate focus, timing or exposure. **Example:** choose a less compressed JPEG for a detailed scene without confusing this with resolution. **See also:** [compression](#g-compression), [resolution](#g-resolution), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

## R

<a id="g-radio-trigger"></a>
### Radio trigger / channels / groups

A radio system that fires or controls compatible remote flashes. Channels separate communication systems; groups allow selected lights to be adjusted together. **Example:** assign key and background lights to different groups on the same channel. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [TTL](#g-ttl), [hot shoe](#g-hot-shoe). [6](#ref-6)

<a id="g-rangefinder"></a>
### Rangefinder

A system that estimates subject distance, traditionally using coincident or split images coupled to lens focus. Its viewing path is usually separate from the taking lens. **Example:** align a double image in the finder to focus a film camera. **See also:** [parallax](#g-parallax), [viewfinder](#g-viewfinder), [manual focus](#g-manual-focus).

<a id="g-raw"></a>
### RAW / raw file

A file retaining sensor-derived capture data and metadata for later interpretation. RAW is a category, not one universal format; files may be compressed or receive camera processing. **Example:** revise white balance during conversion while retaining the original capture file. **See also:** [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [DNG](#g-dng), [demosaicing](#g-demosaicing), [non-destructive editing](#g-non-destructive-editing). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — File choices](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#specifications-that-matter). [12](#ref-12)

<a id="g-rear-curtain-sync"></a>
### Rear-curtain / slow sync

Rear-curtain sync fires the principal flash near the end of exposure. Slow sync combines flash with a relatively long shutter time to retain ambient light. They can be used together. **Example:** a moving subject has an ambient trail behind its flash-defined final position. **See also:** [flash synchronisation](#g-flash-synchronisation), [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [flash](#g-flash).

<a id="g-reciprocity-failure"></a>
### Reciprocity failure

A departure from the usual trade-off between light intensity and exposure time in photographic materials. Long film exposures may require additional time and, for some materials, colour correction. **Example:** consult the film's data sheet when a meter recommends a lengthy night exposure. **See also:** [long exposure](#g-long-exposure), [exposure](#g-exposure), [development](#g-development). [13](#ref-13)

<a id="g-recycle-time"></a>
### Recycle time

The time a flash needs to become ready for another exposure at a specified output. It depends on output level, power supply and thermal conditions. **Example:** a rapid sequence outruns the flash, leaving some frames without adequate flash illumination. **See also:** [flash](#g-flash), [burst mode](#g-burst-mode), [flash duration](#g-flash-duration).

<a id="g-reflected-metering"></a>
### Reflected metering

Measuring light reflected from the scene towards the meter. Readings depend on subject reflectance as well as illumination. **Example:** the same lighting can yield different readings from a white wall and a black coat. **See also:** [incident metering](#g-incident-metering), [metering](#g-metering), [exposure compensation](#g-exposure-compensation).

<a id="g-reflector"></a>
### Reflector

A surface that redirects light. White, silver, gold and other surfaces differ in brightness, directionality and colour effects. **Example:** place white card below a small product to brighten its underside. **See also:** [fill light](#g-fill-light), [bounce flash](#g-bounce-flash), [negative fill](#g-negative-fill). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Accessories](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#other-accessories).

<a id="g-remote-release"></a>
### Remote release / self-timer

A remote release triggers the camera without pressing its body-mounted shutter button. A self-timer delays capture after activation. **Example:** use a two-second delay to let tripod vibrations settle before a still-life exposure. **See also:** [tripod](#g-tripod), [camera shake](#g-camera-shake), [bulb](#g-bulb).

<a id="g-rendering-intent"></a>
### Rendering intent

A strategy for mapping colours between colour spaces, particularly when the destination cannot reproduce all source colours. Results depend on profiles and software. **Example:** compare perceptual and relative colorimetric conversions when soft-proofing vivid flowers for print. **See also:** [gamut](#g-gamut), [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile), [soft proofing](#g-soft-proofing). [7](#ref-7)

<a id="g-representation"></a>
### Representation

How people, places or ideas are depicted and made meaningful. Photographs are shaped by choices and relationships, even when they describe real events. **Example:** a low viewpoint presents a person differently from a distant, elevated view. **See also:** [context](#g-context), [viewpoint](#g-viewpoint), [consent](#g-consent). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Influential photographers](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#photographers-who-changed-what-a-photograph-could-be).

<a id="g-reproduction-ratio"></a>
### Reproduction ratio / magnification

The ratio of the image's physical size on the recording surface to the subject's actual size. It is independent of later screen or print enlargement. **Example:** at 1:2, a 20 mm object forms a 10 mm image. **See also:** [macro photography](#g-macro-photography), [close-up](#g-close-up), [extension tube](#g-extension-tube).

<a id="g-resampling"></a>
### Resampling / resizing

Resampling changes pixel dimensions by estimating or combining samples. Resizing can also mean changing physical print dimensions without changing pixels. **Example:** reduce 6000 × 4000 pixels to 3000 × 2000 for an output copy. **See also:** [interpolation](#g-interpolation), [PPI](#g-ppi), [export](#g-export).

<a id="g-resolution"></a>
### Resolution

The ability to distinguish detail, or, in some specifications, an image's pixel dimensions or output sampling density. State which meaning is intended. **Example:** a 24 MP file can contain little resolved detail if focus was missed. **See also:** [megapixel](#g-megapixel), [sharpness](#g-sharpness), [PPI](#g-ppi), [MTF](#g-mtf).

<a id="g-retouching"></a>
### Retouching

Local changes to remove, repair or reshape image details. The term covers small corrections and substantial alterations, whose acceptability depends on context. **Example:** remove sensor-dust spots from a landscape file. **See also:** [image manipulation](#g-image-manipulation), [mask](#g-mask), [post-processing](#g-post-processing).

<a id="g-rgb"></a>
### RGB

Red, green and blue channels used in additive colour representation. RGB numbers require a defined colour space to identify colours unambiguously. **Example:** a display combines emitted red, green and blue light to form a colour image. **See also:** [colour space](#g-colour-space), [CMYK](#g-cmyk), [sRGB](#g-srgb).

<a id="g-rights-managed"></a>
### Rights-managed

A licensing approach in which the price and permission are tied to specified uses. The contract determines the actual scope. **Example:** license an image for a defined print run in one territory for a fixed period. **See also:** [licence](#g-licence), [royalty-free](#g-royalty-free), [copyright](#g-copyright).

<a id="g-rim-light"></a>
### Rim light / hair light

Light placed to create a bright edge or illuminate hair, helping distinguish a subject from its background. **Example:** place a controlled light behind and to the side of a dark-haired sitter. **See also:** [backlighting](#g-backlighting), [subject separation](#g-subject-separation), [grid](#g-grid).

<a id="g-roll-film"></a>
### Roll film / 35 mm / 120

Film supplied on a roll. “35 mm” describes a film width, commonly giving 24 × 36 mm still frames; 120 is a roll-film designation, not a 120 mm width. **Example:** one 120 camera records square frames while another records rectangular ones. **See also:** [medium format](#g-medium-format), [full frame](#g-full-frame), [format](#g-format).

<a id="g-rolling-shutter"></a>
### Rolling shutter

Capture in which different image rows or regions integrate light at different times. Subject or camera motion can then distort geometry. Mechanical focal-plane shutters also traverse the frame over time. **Example:** a fast pan makes vertical poles lean. **See also:** [electronic shutter](#g-electronic-shutter), [global shutter](#g-global-shutter), [banding](#g-banding).

<a id="g-royalty-free"></a>
### Royalty-free

A licence model commonly allowing repeated uses within stated terms without a separate royalty for each use. It does not mean free of charge, unrestricted or outside copyright. **Example:** purchase a stock-image licence permitting several publications subject to its conditions. **See also:** [licence](#g-licence), [rights-managed](#g-rights-managed), [public domain](#g-public-domain).

<a id="g-rule-of-thirds"></a>
### Rule of thirds

A compositional guide dividing the frame into three equal parts horizontally and vertically. Placing elements near those lines can be useful, but does not guarantee a strong image. **Example:** compare a horizon near the lower third with one centred in the frame. **See also:** [composition](#g-composition), [balance](#g-balance), [symmetry](#g-symmetry).

## S

<a id="g-safelight"></a>
### Safelight

A light source chosen for use with particular photographic materials under specified conditions. It is not safe for every film or paper, nor indefinitely. **Example:** handle compatible black-and-white printing paper under its recommended safelight while loading most camera film in total darkness. **See also:** [darkroom](#g-darkroom), [development](#g-development).

<a id="g-salted-paper-print"></a>
### Salted-paper print

An early photographic paper-printing process with a silver image formed in paper treated with salt and silver nitrate. It differs from a paper negative used to make such a print. **Example:** a historical paper negative is contact-printed onto salted paper. **See also:** [negative](#g-negative), [albumen print](#g-albumen-print), [contact print](#g-contact-sheet). [17](#ref-17)

<a id="g-saturation"></a>
### Saturation / vibrance

Saturation concerns colourfulness relative to brightness in a given model. Vibrance is an application-dependent adjustment that commonly treats less-saturated colours differently. **Example:** increase muted landscape colours while checking that already vivid flowers retain believable detail. **See also:** [hue](#g-hue), [luminance](#g-luminance), [clipping](#g-clipping).

<a id="g-scanning"></a>
### Scanning / digitisation

Creating a digital representation of a physical photograph, negative or transparency. Focus, illumination, colour interpretation and handling affect the result. **Example:** digitise a negative with a scanner or a camera and even backlighting. **See also:** [negative](#g-negative), [resolution](#g-resolution), [archival](#g-archival).

<a id="g-scheimpflug-principle"></a>
### Scheimpflug principle

A geometric relationship used to understand the tilted plane of focus when lens and recording planes are not parallel. Tilt changes focus-plane orientation rather than making everything sharp. **Example:** align focus along an angled tabletop with a suitable camera or lens movement. **See also:** [tilt-shift lens](#g-tilt-shift-lens), [large format](#g-large-format), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field).

<a id="g-scientific-photography"></a>
### Scientific / technical photography

Photography made for observation, measurement, documentation or analysis. Consistency, scale and traceable procedures may be more important than expressive effects. **Example:** record an object under repeatable lighting with a scale reference. **See also:** [reproduction ratio](#g-reproduction-ratio), [metadata](#g-metadata), [calibration](#g-calibration). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Movement and scale](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#movement-and-scale-sport-wildlife-and-close-up-work).

<a id="g-selective-focus"></a>
### Selective focus

Using focus placement and limited depth of field to emphasise a chosen part of a scene. **Example:** focus on one face in a crowd while allowing others to soften. **See also:** [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [subject separation](#g-subject-separation), [bokeh](#g-bokeh).

<a id="g-sensitometry"></a>
### Sensitometry / characteristic curve

The study of how photographic materials respond to exposure and processing. A characteristic curve relates log exposure to resulting density. **Example:** compare how two development methods change negative contrast. **See also:** [density](#g-density), [development](#g-development), [exposure latitude](#g-exposure-latitude).

<a id="g-sensor"></a>
### Sensor / CMOS / CCD

A device converting incident light into electrical signals used to form a digital image. CMOS and CCD describe different sensor architectures; neither name alone determines image quality. **Example:** photons collected during an exposure contribute to measured signals at sensor sites. **See also:** [pixel](#g-pixel), [sensor size](#g-sensor-size), [noise](#g-noise).

<a id="g-sensor-size"></a>
### Sensor size

The recording sensor's physical dimensions, distinct from pixel count. Format affects angle of view for a given lens and influences system-level trade-offs. **Example:** full frame and APS-C cameras can both record 24 MP while using different physical areas. **See also:** [crop factor](#g-crop-factor), [full frame](#g-full-frame), [megapixel](#g-megapixel). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Specifications](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#specifications-that-matter).

<a id="g-sequence"></a>
### Sequence / sequencing

The order and relationships of photographs in a presentation. Repetition, contrast, pacing and transitions can create meaning beyond individual frames. **Example:** place an empty-room photograph after a crowded celebration to change the project's emotional rhythm. **See also:** [photo essay](#g-photo-essay), [photobook](#g-photobook), [context](#g-context). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Circulation](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#the-public-and-private-lives-of-photographs).

<a id="g-sharpening"></a>
### Sharpening

Processing that increases apparent edge definition. It can improve perceived crispness but does not generally restore all information lost to blur. Appropriate output sharpening depends on size and medium. **Example:** compare a print after applying sharpening suited to its final dimensions. **See also:** [sharpness](#g-sharpness), [halo](#g-halo), [noise reduction](#g-noise-reduction).

<a id="g-sharpness"></a>
### Sharpness / acutance

Sharpness is the impression of clear detail, influenced by resolution, edge contrast, focus, movement and viewing. Acutance describes edge-transition crispness. **Example:** a high-contrast edge can look crisp even when fine texture is unresolved. **See also:** [resolution](#g-resolution), [MTF](#g-mtf), [sharpening](#g-sharpening), [focus](#g-focus).

<a id="g-shutter-priority"></a>
### Shutter priority (S / Tv)

A mode in which you choose shutter speed and the camera chooses aperture, with ISO also adjustable automatically if enabled. Lens limits can prevent the requested exposure. **Example:** select 1/1000 s for action and check whether aperture or ISO reaches its limit. **See also:** [aperture priority](#g-aperture-priority), [Auto ISO](#g-auto-iso), [shutter speed](#g-shutter-speed).

<a id="g-shutter-speed"></a>
### Shutter speed / exposure time

The duration over which the recording surface receives light for an exposure. Faster means shorter, not a larger fraction. **Example:** 1/1000 s is shorter than 1/125 s and admits one-eighth as much light at the same aperture. **See also:** [stop](#g-stop), [motion blur](#g-motion-blur), [flash duration](#g-flash-duration). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Shutter speed](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#shutter-speed-time-and-movement).

<a id="g-sidecar-file"></a>
### Sidecar file

A companion file storing information associated with an image, often metadata or processing instructions. It should be kept associated with its original. **Example:** move a RAW file together with its matching XMP sidecar. **See also:** [XMP](#g-xmp), [metadata](#g-metadata), [non-destructive editing](#g-non-destructive-editing).

<a id="g-side-lighting"></a>
### Side lighting / front lighting

Side lighting reaches the subject from a lateral direction and often reveals form and texture. Front lighting comes from near the camera's direction and can minimise visible shadows. **Example:** move a lamp sideways to reveal raised lettering. **See also:** [key light](#g-key-light), [backlighting](#g-backlighting), [contrast](#g-contrast).

<a id="g-signal-to-noise-ratio"></a>
### Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)

The strength of useful signal relative to its fluctuations or unwanted noise, under a stated measurement. More captured light generally improves photon-limited SNR. **Example:** a longer exposure of a stationary dim scene records cleaner shadows when highlights remain within range. **See also:** [noise](#g-noise), [exposure](#g-exposure), [stacking](#g-stacking).

<a id="g-silhouette"></a>
### Silhouette

A subject shown predominantly as a dark shape against a brighter background. Its recognisable outline often matters more than internal detail. **Example:** expose for a bright sunset sky and let a person's figure remain dark. **See also:** [backlighting](#g-backlighting), [negative space](#g-negative-space), [exposure](#g-exposure).

<a id="g-single-autofocus"></a>
### Single autofocus (AF-S / One-Shot)

A mode that acquires focus once per activation and commonly holds it while activation continues. Brand labels vary. **Example:** focus on a stationary sculpture before releasing the shutter. **See also:** [continuous autofocus](#g-continuous-autofocus), [focus lock](#g-focus-lock), [autofocus](#g-autofocus).

<a id="g-slide-film"></a>
### Slide / reversal film

Film processed to produce a positive transparency. It can be viewed by transmitted light, projected or digitised. **Example:** inspect a colour slide on a light box. **See also:** [negative](#g-negative), [exposure latitude](#g-exposure-latitude), [scanning](#g-scanning).

<a id="g-smartphone-photography"></a>
### Smartphone photography

Image-making with a phone's camera system, often combining multiple modules and substantial automatic processing. Optical and software controls vary. **Example:** tap a face to guide focus and adjust brightness before capture. **See also:** [computational photography](#g-computational-photography), [portrait mode](#g-portrait-mode), [digital zoom](#g-digital-zoom). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Camera types](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#main-types-of-cameras).

<a id="g-softbox"></a>
### Softbox / umbrella

Modifiers that redistribute a light source, often creating a larger illuminated area. Softboxes generally offer more controlled spill; umbrella behaviour depends on design and placement. **Example:** place a large softbox near a sitter for broad, controlled illumination. **See also:** [soft light](#g-soft-light), [diffuser](#g-diffuser), [grid](#g-grid).

<a id="g-soft-light"></a>
### Soft light

Light producing gradual shadow transitions, usually from a source large in apparent size from the subject. A large source moved farther away becomes relatively smaller and harder. **Example:** a nearby broad window gives softer facial shadows than a small bare lamp. **See also:** [hard light](#g-hard-light), [diffuser](#g-diffuser), [softbox](#g-softbox).

<a id="g-soft-proofing"></a>
### Soft proofing

Simulating an output condition on a display using profiles and supported viewing options. It helps anticipate changes but cannot fully reproduce paper, surface or viewing light. **Example:** preview how a saturated blue may change on matte paper. **See also:** [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile), [gamut](#g-gamut), [test print](#g-test-print).

<a id="g-sports-photography"></a>
### Sports / action photography

Photography concerned with sporting activity or movement, including anticipation, atmosphere and reactions. Freezing motion is one possible treatment. **Example:** choose a fast shutter for a jump or pan to express speed. **See also:** [panning](#g-panning), [continuous autofocus](#g-continuous-autofocus), [burst mode](#g-burst-mode). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Movement and scale](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#movement-and-scale-sport-wildlife-and-close-up-work).

<a id="g-srgb"></a>
### sRGB

A widely used RGB colour space designed for interoperable image exchange. It is commonly suitable for web delivery, although platforms and output requirements differ. **Example:** convert an edited master to a profile-tagged sRGB JPEG for a conventional website. **See also:** [colour space](#g-colour-space), [working colour space](#g-working-colour-space), [export](#g-export).

<a id="g-stacking"></a>
### Stacking

Combining multiple frames for a chosen purpose, such as extended focus, noise reduction or star trails. Different stacks require different capture and merging methods. **Example:** align and average repeated night-sky frames to reduce random noise. **See also:** [focus stacking](#g-focus-stacking), [HDR](#g-hdr), [astrophotography](#g-astrophotography).

<a id="g-staging"></a>
### Staging / tableau

Arranging subjects or actions for the camera. A tableau is a constructed scene, often with theatrical or narrative qualities. **Example:** pose figures in an interior to suggest a fictional encounter. **See also:** [candid](#g-candid), [fine art photography](#g-fine-art-photography), [representation](#g-representation).

<a id="g-still-life"></a>
### Still life / product / food photography

Still life concerns arranged inanimate subjects. Product and food photography often apply this control to particular descriptive or commercial purposes. **Example:** reposition a light and flag until a glass bottle's reflections describe its form. **See also:** [commercial photography](#g-commercial-photography), [flag](#g-flag), [tethering](#g-tethering).

<a id="g-stock-photography"></a>
### Stock photography

Photographs offered for licensing from an existing collection rather than created solely for one new commission. Permitted uses depend on each image's licence. **Example:** an editor licenses an existing mountain image for an article. **See also:** [rights-managed](#g-rights-managed), [royalty-free](#g-royalty-free), [licence](#g-licence).

<a id="g-stop"></a>
### Stop / stopping down / opening up

A stop is a factor-of-two change in light or the corresponding exposure-setting step. Stopping down means making the aperture smaller; opening up means larger. **Example:** f/4 to f/5.6 reduces light by one stop, offset by changing 1/250 s to 1/125 s. **See also:** [f-number](#g-f-number), [EV](#g-ev), [exposure](#g-exposure).

<a id="g-street-photography"></a>
### Street photography

Photography observing everyday life and relationships in shared spaces. The approach is not confined to streets and does not remove responsibilities towards depicted people. **Example:** recognise a brief alignment of a passer-by, reflection and poster. **See also:** [candid](#g-candid), [decisive moment](#g-decisive-moment), [consent](#g-consent). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — People](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#people-encounter-observation-and-performance).

<a id="g-subject-detection"></a>
### Subject detection / eye AF

Systems that identify subject types or features to help place and track focus. Detection does not guarantee focus on the intended subject or eye. **Example:** confirm which person the camera selected in a crowded portrait. **See also:** [AF area](#g-af-area), [continuous autofocus](#g-continuous-autofocus), [autofocus](#g-autofocus).

<a id="g-subject-separation"></a>
### Subject separation

Making a subject distinguishable from its surroundings through tone, colour, lighting, focus or placement. Background blur is one method among several. **Example:** place a dark-haired sitter against a light wall instead of simply opening the aperture. **See also:** [selective focus](#g-selective-focus), [rim light](#g-rim-light), [negative space](#g-negative-space).

<a id="g-symmetry"></a>
### Symmetry / pattern

Symmetry organises corresponding elements around an axis or centre; a pattern repeats elements. Either can establish order, with interruptions creating emphasis. **Example:** one open window interrupts a repeated grid of closed shutters. **See also:** [balance](#g-balance), [composition](#g-composition), [rule of thirds](#g-rule-of-thirds).

## T

<a id="g-teleconverter"></a>
### Teleconverter

An optical accessory between lens and camera that increases effective focal length while reducing maximum effective aperture. Compatibility and image quality vary. **Example:** a 1.4× converter turns a 200 mm f/2.8 combination into approximately 280 mm f/4. **See also:** [telephoto lens](#g-telephoto-lens), [extension tube](#g-extension-tube), [maximum aperture](#g-maximum-aperture).

<a id="g-telephoto-lens"></a>
### Telephoto / long lens

In common photographic use, a lens giving a narrow view and large subject scale from a distance. Strictly, telephoto describes an optical design shorter than its effective focal length. **Example:** use 300 mm on full frame to frame a distant animal tightly. **See also:** [angle of view](#g-angle-of-view), [perspective compression](#g-perspective-compression), [teleconverter](#g-teleconverter).

<a id="g-test-print"></a>
### Test print / test strip

A trial output used to assess tone, colour, detail or exposure before making the final print. Darkroom test strips commonly compare exposure times on one sheet. **Example:** inspect a small section at final scale before printing an exhibition photograph. **See also:** [soft proofing](#g-soft-proofing), [darkroom](#g-darkroom), [PPI](#g-ppi).

<a id="g-tethering"></a>
### Tethering / tethered capture

Connecting a camera to another device for image transfer, viewing or control during shooting. Supported functions depend on equipment and software. **Example:** inspect product detail on a computer as each frame arrives. **See also:** [still life](#g-still-life), [live view](#g-live-view), [workflow](#g-workflow). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Commercial work](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#evidence-persuasion-and-invention).

<a id="g-texture"></a>
### Texture

The visible appearance of a surface's fine structure. Light direction, contrast, focus and scale affect how strongly it is described. **Example:** grazing side light makes wrinkles in paper more visible. **See also:** [side lighting](#g-side-lighting), [contrast](#g-contrast), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-thumbnail"></a>
### Thumbnail / preview

A smaller representation used for browsing or viewing. A preview may have different resolution, processing or colour handling from the original. **Example:** an apparently sharp thumbnail reveals missed focus when the original is enlarged. **See also:** [catalogue](#g-catalogue), [RAW](#g-raw), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-tiff"></a>
### TIFF

A flexible raster-image format that can support high bit depth, metadata and lossless compression. Supported features vary between applications. **Example:** supply a 16-bit TIFF to a retoucher who requests a rendered file with substantial editing latitude. **See also:** [JPEG](#g-jpeg), [bit depth](#g-bit-depth), [lossless](#g-lossless).

<a id="g-tilt-shift-lens"></a>
### Tilt-shift lens / camera movements

A lens or camera arrangement allowing tilt and shift. Tilt changes focus-plane orientation; shift changes framing relative to the image circle and can help keep the camera level. **Example:** shift upwards to include a building without tilting the camera. **See also:** [converging verticals](#g-converging-verticals), [Scheimpflug principle](#g-scheimpflug-principle), [image circle](#g-image-circle).

<a id="g-time-lapse"></a>
### Time-lapse / intervalometer

Time-lapse presents frames captured at intervals in a faster sequence, making slow change visible. An intervalometer controls repeated capture timing. **Example:** photograph a plant every ten minutes, then play the frames as a short movie. **See also:** [frames per second](#g-frames-per-second), [remote release](#g-remote-release), [long exposure](#g-long-exposure).

<a id="g-tint"></a>
### Tint

In many RAW editors, the green–magenta component of white-balance adjustment, complementary to the temperature control. More generally, a tint can mean an added colour bias. **Example:** reduce a fluorescent-lit portrait's green cast using tint adjustment. **See also:** [white balance](#g-white-balance), [colour temperature](#g-colour-temperature), [colour cast](#g-colour-cast).

<a id="g-tone"></a>
### Tone / tonal range

Tone describes relative lightness or darkness; tonal range is the span and distribution of tones in an image. Tonality also includes their transitions. **Example:** retain gentle midtone differences in a misty scene instead of forcing deep blacks. **See also:** [contrast](#g-contrast), [highlights](#g-highlights), [curves](#g-curves).

<a id="g-tone-mapping"></a>
### Tone mapping

Mapping a source brightness range into a different output range, often to display an HDR capture on a more limited medium. The mapping can be global or locally adaptive. **Example:** compress a bright window's tones while retaining a readable interior. **See also:** [HDR](#g-hdr), [dynamic range](#g-dynamic-range), [halo](#g-halo).

<a id="g-toning"></a>
### Toning / colour grading

Toning changes a monochrome image's colour; chemical toning may also alter image materials and stability. Colour grading shapes the colour relationships and mood of an image or series. **Example:** introduce cool shadows and warm highlights for a deliberate visual treatment. **See also:** [black-and-white](#g-black-and-white), [colour cast](#g-colour-cast), [post-processing](#g-post-processing).

<a id="g-tripod"></a>
### Tripod / monopod

A tripod supports the camera on three legs; a monopod offers one-legged support with greater mobility. Neither stops subject movement. **Example:** use a tripod for a long still-life exposure and a monopod to support a heavy lens at a match. **See also:** [camera shake](#g-camera-shake), [remote release](#g-remote-release), [image stabilisation](#g-image-stabilisation).

<a id="g-t-stop"></a>
### T-stop

A lens-aperture measure adjusted for actual light transmission. It is especially useful when matching exposure between lenses. It does not replace f-number for depth-of-field calculations. **Example:** two lenses at the same T-stop aim to deliver matching light levels under equivalent conditions. **See also:** [f-number](#g-f-number), [aperture](#g-aperture), [exposure](#g-exposure).

<a id="g-ttl"></a>
### TTL (through the lens)

A description of measurement through the taking lens. In automatic flash systems, TTL commonly uses a measured preflash to determine output, although implementations differ. **Example:** TTL adjusts flash as the subject-to-flash distance changes. **See also:** [flash exposure compensation](#g-flash-exposure-compensation), [flash](#g-flash), [metering](#g-metering). [6](#ref-6)

## U

<a id="g-unsharp-mask"></a>
### Unsharp mask

A sharpening method that uses differences between an image and a blurred version to emphasise edges. Its name comes from a photographic masking technique. **Example:** apply a restrained amount and radius while checking for bright outlines. **See also:** [sharpening](#g-sharpening), [halo](#g-halo), [sharpness](#g-sharpness).

<a id="g-upscaling"></a>
### Upscaling / super resolution

Increasing image pixel dimensions using interpolation or more elaborate reconstruction. Some methods combine frames; learned methods may infer plausible detail that is not a reliable record of the subject. **Example:** enlarge a low-resolution file for a display while inspecting text and fine patterns for errors. **See also:** [interpolation](#g-interpolation), [resampling](#g-resampling), [resolution](#g-resolution).

## V

<a id="g-viewfinder"></a>
### Viewfinder (OVF / EVF)

A viewing system used to frame the photograph. An optical viewfinder shows an optical scene; an electronic viewfinder displays an electronic image. Neither automatically guarantees exact final exposure or framing. **Example:** use an EVF's magnification to check manual focus. **See also:** [DSLR](#g-dslr), [mirrorless camera](#g-mirrorless-camera), [live view](#g-live-view).

<a id="g-viewpoint"></a>
### Viewpoint / vantage point

The camera's position relative to the scene, including height and distance. Changing position changes perspective and the relationships between elements. **Example:** crouch to place a flower against sky instead of pavement. **See also:** [perspective](#g-perspective), [composition](#g-composition), [framing](#g-framing).

<a id="g-vignetting"></a>
### Vignetting

A reduction in brightness towards image edges. It can arise from lens behaviour, obstructing accessories or deliberate processing. **Example:** stacked filters produce dark corners on a wide-angle lens. **See also:** [lens correction](#g-lens-correction), [image circle](#g-image-circle), [lens hood](#g-lens-hood).

<a id="g-visualisation"></a>
### Visualisation / previsualisation

Imagining a finished photograph and using that intention to guide capture and processing. The intention can concern tone, arrangement, material or presentation. **Example:** plan a dark, quiet print before choosing exposure and later tonal treatment. **See also:** [fine art photography](#g-fine-art-photography), [tone](#g-tone), [test print](#g-test-print). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Influential photographers](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#photographers-who-changed-what-a-photograph-could-be).

## W

<a id="g-weather-sealing"></a>
### Weather sealing

Construction intended to resist specified environmental exposure, such as dust or moisture. It is not synonymous with waterproofing, and protection depends on the complete equipment combination. **Example:** check the camera and lens specifications before working in sustained rain. **See also:** [lens mount](#g-lens-mount), [wildlife photography](#g-wildlife-photography).

<a id="g-wedding-photography"></a>
### Wedding / event photography

Photography covering a planned occasion through a mixture of observation and organised pictures. Timing, relationships and reliable delivery are central. **Example:** move from candid ceremony moments to a carefully arranged group portrait. **See also:** [candid](#g-candid), [portrait photography](#g-portrait-photography), [mixed lighting](#g-mixed-lighting). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — People](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#people-encounter-observation-and-performance).

<a id="g-wet-plate-collodion"></a>
### Wet-plate collodion

A historical process using a collodion-coated plate that is sensitised, exposed and developed while still wet. Variants produce negatives or direct-positive appearances on different supports. **Example:** a tintype portrait presents an image on a dark metal support. **See also:** [alternative process](#g-alternative-process), [daguerreotype](#g-daguerreotype), [negative](#g-negative). [9](#ref-9)

<a id="g-white-balance"></a>
### White balance (WB / AWB)

Adjusting colour interpretation so a chosen neutral reference appears neutral, or deliberately departing from neutrality for expression. AWB means automatic white balance. **Example:** set a reference under the subject's light to reduce an unwanted warm cast. **See also:** [colour temperature](#g-colour-temperature), [tint](#g-tint), [mixed lighting](#g-mixed-lighting), [RAW](#g-raw).

<a id="g-wide-angle-lens"></a>
### Wide-angle lens

A lens giving a broader view than a standard lens on the chosen format. Close viewpoints can exaggerate near–far relationships, but viewpoint causes that perspective effect. **Example:** use 24 mm on full frame to include an interior and move carefully to manage edge relationships. **See also:** [angle of view](#g-angle-of-view), [perspective](#g-perspective), [fisheye lens](#g-fisheye-lens).

<a id="g-wildlife-photography"></a>
### Wildlife photography

Photography of animals and their relationships with habitat and behaviour. Knowledge, patience and avoiding disturbance support both the work and its subjects. **Example:** wait at a suitable distance for natural behaviour rather than provoking movement. **See also:** [telephoto lens](#g-telephoto-lens), [continuous autofocus](#g-continuous-autofocus), [environmental portrait](#g-environmental-portrait). **In the book:** [Chapter 5 — Movement and scale](05_Photography_genres_%26_careers.md#movement-and-scale-sport-wildlife-and-close-up-work).

<a id="g-workflow"></a>
### Workflow

The connected steps from preparation and capture through selection, processing, delivery and preservation. Clear routines reduce lost files and inconsistent results. **Example:** ingest, verify copies, add metadata, select, edit, export and back up a completed assignment. **See also:** [digital asset management](#g-digital-asset-management), [export](#g-export), [backup](#g-backup).

<a id="g-working-colour-space"></a>
### Working colour space / Adobe RGB / ProPhoto RGB

The colour space used for editing or intermediate files. Adobe RGB and ProPhoto RGB have larger gamuts than sRGB in important regions, but require suitable colour management and precision. **Example:** retain a high-bit-depth wide-gamut master and make a separate sRGB delivery copy. **See also:** [colour space](#g-colour-space), [bit depth](#g-bit-depth), [sRGB](#g-srgb).

## X

<a id="g-xmp"></a>
### XMP (Extensible Metadata Platform)

A framework for encoding and exchanging metadata. XMP can be embedded in a file or stored in a sidecar; it can carry IPTC properties and application-specific instructions. **Example:** an XMP sidecar stores a RAW photograph's keywords and adjustment settings. **See also:** [metadata](#g-metadata), [IPTC](#g-iptc), [sidecar file](#g-sidecar-file). ## Z

<a id="g-zone-focusing"></a>
### Zone focusing

Pre-setting focus and aperture to cover an anticipated range of subject distances. It relies on depth-of-field assumptions rather than tracking every subject. **Example:** wait for a passer-by to enter a prepared sharp zone. **See also:** [hyperfocal distance](#g-hyperfocal-distance), [depth of field](#g-depth-of-field), [street photography](#g-street-photography).

<a id="g-zone-system"></a>
### Zone System

An approach developed by Ansel Adams and Fred Archer connecting visualised tones with exposure, negative development and printing. Its zone diagrams guide tonal placement rather than specify a universal sensor dynamic range. **Example:** decide how dark a textured shadow should appear, then relate metering and processing to that intention. **See also:** [visualisation](#g-visualisation), [spot metering](#g-metering), [sensitometry](#g-sensitometry).

<a id="g-zoom-lens"></a>
### Zoom lens

A lens with an adjustable focal length. Optical zoom changes the projected view before capture; the zoom ratio divides longest focal length by shortest. **Example:** 24–120 mm is a 5× zoom, without specifying how it compares with another format. **See also:** [prime lens](#g-prime-lens), [digital zoom](#g-digital-zoom), [maximum aperture](#g-maximum-aperture). **In the book:** [Chapter 3 — Lens types](03_equipment_%26_settings.md#focal-length-and-lens-types).

| [Exposure](#g-exposure) and brightness | Capture exposure concerns received light; displayed brightness also depends on signal handling and rendering. |

| [Resolution](#g-resolution) and [sharpness](#g-sharpness) | Resolving fine detail and looking crisp are related but different qualities. |
| [Camera shake](#g-camera-shake) and [subject motion](#g-motion-blur) | Stabilisation and support address camera movement; a moving subject needs a suitable exposure strategy. |
| [Focal length](#g-focal-length) and [perspective](#g-perspective) | Focal length changes the view; camera position changes relative spatial relationships. |
| [Distortion](#g-distortion) and [converging verticals](#g-converging-verticals) | Curved-line lens mapping differs from the perspective of a tilted camera. |
| [DPI](#g-dpi) and [PPI](#g-ppi) | Printer dots differ from the image samples allocated per inch of output. |
| [Colour space](#g-colour-space) and [ICC profile](#g-icc-profile) | A colour space defines a representation; a profile supplies information for its interpretation or conversion. |
| [Exif](#g-exif), [IPTC](#g-iptc) and [XMP](#g-xmp) | Capture-oriented metadata standards, descriptive properties and metadata encoding frameworks have different roles that can overlap. |
| [Copyright](#g-copyright), [credit](#g-credit) and [licence](#g-licence) | Rights, attribution and permission to use an image are separate matters. |
